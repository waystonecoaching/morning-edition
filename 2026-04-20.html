Missouri House passes bill to study psilocybin therapy for vets and first responders

State-level legislation naming vets and first responders as the study population is rare and worth tracking. It signals pressure on clinical pathways that serve exactly the people Mark works with, and it names psilocybin specifically rather than psychedelics as a category. Scope-of-practice edge: the study frame is clinical research, not a coaching or integration mandate. Worth watching whether any Missouri integration-provider pathway emerges downstream.

The Missouri House advanced a bill that would authorize a state-funded study of psilocybin-assisted therapy for veterans and first responders with PTSD. It is a study bill, not a treatment-access bill. Sponsors frame it as a response to suicide rates in the responder and veteran populations. Bill text would route through a designated research pathway if the Senate concurs.

Try this: Skim the bill text when it posts. Note the study design and the listed research partners; that tells you which practitioner lanes are being built.

State of the Science: MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for the treatment of posttraumatic stress disorder

Peer-reviewed review in the main trauma journal, post-Lykos FDA rejection. This is the academic read Mark needs when a client or a colleague cites advocacy press. The field is under credibility stress, and a state-of-the-science piece either sharpens or soft-pedals that stress. Either way, Mark should know what this one says before the next integration conversation.

The review synthesizes evidence on MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for PTSD through the current cycle of trials, the FDA rejection of the Lykos submission, and the open methodological questions around blinding, therapist adherence, and long-term outcomes. It treats the evidence base as promising and incomplete. The authors distinguish efficacy signal from readiness for standard-of-care adoption. It is the kind of piece that belongs in the bibliography of any practitioner working near this population.

Try this: Pull the PDF, read the methods and limitations sections first, and save a short note in the Wiki for future client questions.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

Texas panel to study firefighter mental health and suicide prevention under new law

A neighboring state building an official firefighter MH panel signals where the policy machinery is heading. Watch what membership looks like: clinicians only, or peers and chaplains at the table. The panels that produce field-usable output are the ones that include working responders and peer-support leads, not only clinical voices.

Texas enacted legislation creating a state panel to study firefighter mental health and suicide prevention. The panel is charged with assessing current department programs, identifying gaps, and reporting to the legislature. Reporting timeline and membership structure are defined in the bill. This sits alongside a growing set of state-level firefighter MH working groups, most of which still under-represent peer and chaplain voices at the table.

What London's firefighter mental health numbers are teaching the fire service

The London Fire Brigade's published data is one of the cleaner international datasets on firefighter MH prevalence and service-level response. The piece translates the numbers into operational lessons for US departments. Useful as benchmark when Mark talks with training officers or peer teams about what good looks like at scale.

FireRescue1 walks through the London Fire Brigade's most recent MH prevalence figures and occupational health response. The piece surfaces three operational lessons: early-career exposure drives long-tail risk, peer-support staffing ratios matter more than program existence, and voluntary self-referral rates rise when command explicitly models use. Practical, service-level reading.

Trump orders $50M in research into psychedelic drugs, including ibogaine, for PTSD and TBI

Federal money into ibogaine and psilocybin research for PTSD and TBI is a meaningful signal, but it is research funding, not an approval. Read it alongside the Journal of Traumatic Stress review in slot 02: the credibility bar for clinical adoption has not moved. Ibogaine has known cardiac risk; any responder Mark speaks with who hears the headline and starts searching retreats needs that context.

The executive order directs roughly $50M in federal research funding toward psychedelic therapies, including ibogaine and psilocybin, for PTSD and TBI in veterans and responders. Framed as clearing bureaucratic hurdles on research pathways. No change yet to scheduling or prescribing authority. Industry coverage ran hot; primary sources should be read closely before inferring any near-term standard of care shift.

Understanding why police officers think alcohol works

The piece surveys why officers report that alcohol works for them, then maps those reported effects onto what the nervous system is actually doing. It distinguishes short-term function from long-term cost, and addresses why generic 'drink less' messaging fails. It argues for replacement strategies that match the same functions: downshift, connection, permission to stop scanning. Field-level writing, not a research paper.

If alcohol is the tool that gets you out of sympathetic, take the tool seriously before you try to remove it.

A candid trade-press read on why alcohol keeps winning inside responder culture, told without moralizing. Useful for Mark when working near substance use without crossing into clinical territory. The piece names the functions alcohol serves (downshift, connection, permission to stop carrying the call) and reads those as needs, not pathology. Scope-of-practice edge: substance use with a high-risk population. Name it as such and refer out when indicated.

Introducing advanced accreditations in mentor coaching and coaching supervision

ICF is building a separate credential tier for mentor coaches and coaching supervisors. Mark's credential path as a solo practitioner runs through this territory at some point, especially if Waystone builds a referral or supervision pipeline for other responder-serving coaches. Pricing and supervisor-to-supervisee ratio rules are promised in the next update.

How to prove coaching ROI to corporate sponsors

Not directly about first responder work, but the underlying question, how to report outcomes without breaching confidentiality, maps onto any conversation Mark has with a fire chief, peer-support lead, or union about sponsoring coaching for members. The framework translates.

ICF offers a four-part framework for reporting coaching outcomes to corporate sponsors while preserving client confidentiality: theme-level rather than individual reporting, leading indicators paired with lagging ones, pre/post behavioral self-assessments, and sponsor check-ins that stay outside the coaching dyad. Nuance in the polyvagal theory controversy

Polyvagal theory has taken public criticism from academic physiology. This piece threads the needle: the clinical frame (Deb Dana's work, the three-state map) can remain useful even when specific physiological claims get revised. Worth reading before the next client conversation where Mark describes the framework on his site or in session. Good material for sharpening language without overclaiming.

The piece surveys the current controversy around Porges's physiological claims and argues that the clinical and somatic applications, the three-state framework Dana and others use, stand on their own evidentiary footing even where Porges's original neuroanatomical model is challenged. It separates theory from practice, and flags where practitioners overreach. Measured and useful.

Source: Psychology Today
