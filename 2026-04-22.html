Nuance in the polyvagal theory controversy

Polyvagal theory has been under sustained critique for the anatomical claims about the dorsal and ventral branches. This Psychology Today piece holds space for the clinical utility of Dana's state language even as the evolutionary story is contested. You lean on this framework with clients, so the language used on the Waystone site matters. The distinction to hold: neuroception and state awareness as working descriptions, not fixed neuroanatomy.

The author walks through the anatomical critique of Porges polyvagal theory, where neuroscientists have disputed the dorsal-sympathetic-ventral hierarchy as written. The clinical utility of the state model, they argue, may not rest on whether the evolutionary pathway is exactly right. Felt safety, co-regulation, and the somatic vocabulary remain useful. The piece suggests practitioners separate the clinical framework from the contested neurobiological claims.

Try this: Audit the Waystone site and client handouts for language that stakes clinical outcomes on specific neuroanatomy. Keep the state language. Loosen the biology.
