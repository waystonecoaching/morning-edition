State of the Science: MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for PTSD

This is the peer-reviewed backbone of the MDMA-for-PTSD conversation, in the journal that sets the tone for the trauma field. It matters because federal dollars and state bills are running ahead of the clinical record, and a sober accounting of trial quality, effect sizes, and unresolved methodological questions is exactly what a practitioner working near psychedelic integration needs on hand.

A comprehensive review of where MDMA-assisted psychotherapy actually stands for PTSD: trial results, mechanistic hypotheses, methodological weaknesses flagged during the MAPS submission cycle, and what clinicians should and should not conclude from the current evidence base. The review addresses functional unblinding, therapist variables, adverse event reporting, and the gap between trial conditions and real-world delivery. It does not tell readers what policy should do. It tells them what the science currently supports and where the load-bearing uncertainty sits.

Try this: Save the citation. When a client or referral partner asks whether MDMA therapy works, send this review rather than a news article.

Nuance in the polyvagal theory controversy

Polyvagal theory has been under sustained academic critique for years, and the critique is not trivial. If the nervous-system language on the Waystone site is going to stay honest, the distinction between Porges's original anatomical claims, the clinical frameworks Dana built on top of them, and what contemporary vagal research actually supports needs to be held carefully. This piece tries to sort those layers.

A measured walk through the polyvagal theory debate: where Porges's anatomical claims about the ventral vagal complex have been challenged by evolutionary biologists, where the clinical applications of Dana's work remain useful regardless of the underlying anatomy, and how practitioners can talk about autonomic state, co-regulation, and felt safety without overclaiming. The piece does not dismiss polyvagal-informed practice; it separates the clinical utility from the contested neuroscience.

Try this: Review Waystone copy for any claim that presents the ventral vagal complex as settled evolutionary biology rather than a working clinical frame.
Source: Psychology Today

Missouri House passes bill to study psilocybin therapy for vets and first responders

Another state placing psilocybin therapy for first responders into its own study-and-fund pipeline. Scope-of-practice edge: these bills typically authorize clinical trials, not unsupervised access, and the gap between what the legislation permits and what ends up being offered in communities is where coaching and integration work gets pulled into territory it should not occupy.

The Missouri House has passed a bill directing a study of psilocybin therapy for veterans and first responders, joining a growing list of states establishing formal mechanisms to examine the evidence and, in some cases, fund early clinical access. The measure now moves to the state Senate. The Missouri move fits a pattern visible across red and purple states of defense-adjacent rationales driving psychedelic policy rather than mental health departments leading it.

No. 05 · Psychedelic Therapy · 2025-11-11

Facing trauma under the influence of psychedelics: A phenomenological study with Nova rave survivors

Peer-reviewed phenomenology of people who were actively dosed when the worst thing of their lives happened. This is the uncomfortable edge of the psychedelic safety conversation, and it speaks directly to what integration practitioners actually encounter rather than the clean trial narrative. A useful counterweight to the policy euphoria.

Researchers conducted in-depth phenomenological interviews with survivors of the October 7, 2023 Nova music festival attack in Israel, many of whom were under the effects of MDMA, psilocybin, LSD, or combinations during the attack and its aftermath. The study examines how the psychedelic state shaped encoding, dissociation, meaning-making, and subsequent trauma symptoms. It contributes to a literature that has mostly treated psychedelics as trauma treatment and rarely as the context in which traumatic exposure happened.

Source: Journal of Psychopharmacology

Trump orders $50M research into psychedelic drugs, including ibogaine, for PTSD and TBI

The single largest federal signal in years. Read it for what it does (accelerates FDA review, directs funding, names ibogaine explicitly) and for what it does not do (resolve any of the methodological questions the State of the Science review raises). Expect a training-program land rush and a lot of people marketing integration services who should not be.

The executive order directs $50M toward psychedelic research for PTSD and traumatic brain injury, names ibogaine and psilocybin specifically, and instructs agencies to streamline FDA review pathways. Framing centers on veteran suicide and the treatment gap for responders. Coverage notes the political coalition behind the move spans VA advocates, libertarians, and a subset of the recovery community, while the clinical community remains split on readiness.

Try this: If the Waystone site mentions psychedelic integration, audit the copy this week. Federal coverage will bring curious clients and scope-of-practice risk in equal measure.

No. 07 · Peer Support · 2026-04-15

What London firefighters' mental health numbers are teaching the fire service

Analysis of the London Fire Brigade's internal mental health survey data, what the numbers indicate about presentation rates, help-seeking, and program uptake, and what frontline leaders are taking from it. The reporting notes the gap between stated willingness to use mental health services and actual use, the role of peer conversation in reducing that gap, and the patterns around substance use and sleep.

The culture absorbs what the culture normalizes. Peer conversation closes the gap that policy cannot.

London Fire Brigade has produced some of the cleaner internal data on firefighter mental health outside the U.S. system, and the patterns they are surfacing (what helps, what does not, what the culture actually absorbs) have reliably predicted what American departments would find two years later. Worth the read even without British shift-culture context.

Source: Firefighter Close Calls

The contemplative tradition and the firehouse converge on this point more often than either would admit.

A feature on the resurgence of informal, relational wellness practice in the fire service: shared meals, small acts of attention inside a crew, and the way those practices do work that formal programs often cannot. Built around examples from departments actively rebuilding the kitchen-table culture that modern scheduling, staffing, and acuity have eroded.
