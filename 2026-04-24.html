State of the Science: MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for the treatment of posttraumatic stress disorder

Peer-reviewed synthesis from the journal of record on trauma. After the FDA rejection of Lykos's NDA and the subsequent methodological reckoning, this review is what a scientifically literate first responder coach should cite, not Pollan, not advocacy press. Read with the credibility stress of the field in view.

A formal state-of-the-science review in JTS covering the current evidence base for MDMA-assisted psychotherapy in PTSD. The piece addresses efficacy signals, trial integrity concerns raised in the MAPS and Lykos programs, functional unblinding, and the path forward for regulated therapeutic models.

Try this: If a client asks whether MDMA therapy is proven, the honest answer is: promising, under reconstruction, and not currently FDA-approved. This paper is the citation.

Nuance in the polyvagal theory controversy

Worth your attention because you use polyvagal language on the Waystone site and in client work. The critique of Porges's original anatomical claims has grown; the clinical usefulness of Dana's map has not disappeared. Learning to hold both is the responsible move, and this piece lays out why.

Psychology Today essay walking through the current academic critique of polyvagal theory, separating the neuroanatomical claims (which have taken real hits) from the clinical framework for nervous-system states (which remains widely useful). Argues for keeping what works while being honest about what the theory does and does not prove.

Try this: Audit the polyvagal language on your site and in client handouts. Where you are stating physiology, soften to what is clinically observable. Where you are describing state-shifts, keep the map.
Source: Psychology Today

ACC and PCC performance evaluation to be replaced by enhanced mentor coaching requirement

This is your credentialing path. ICF is removing the recorded performance evaluation at ACC and PCC and routing assessment through expanded mentor coaching hours. Operationally it means more hours with a qualified mentor and one less recorded-session bar. Budget and calendar accordingly.

ICF announced that beginning under the new cycle, the ACC and PCC performance evaluations will be retired and replaced by an enhanced mentor coaching requirement. Candidates will complete expanded mentor hours in lieu of submitting a recorded session for scoring. Full implementation dates and transition rules are laid out on the ICF blog post.

Try this: Before end of week: map the new mentor-hour count against what you have already logged, identify the gap, and line up mentor sessions so the credentialing timeline does not slip.
Source: International Coaching Federation

Moral injury among journalists: a scoping review

Moral injury is your language, even when the population is not yours. The journalist literature is thinner than the military and responder literature, and a scoping review this year says the construct is travelling. Read it as a mirror on your own population: witnessing as a profession, and the cost of bearing witness without the authority to act.

Scoping review in the Journal of Traumatic Stress mapping the current literature on moral injury in working journalists. Identifies distinct injury patterns tied to witnessing and reporting on atrocity, institutional constraint, and perceived betrayal by employers. Calls for population-specific measures and interventions rather than importing combat-derived frameworks wholesale.

Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress
