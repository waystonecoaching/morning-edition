Posttraumatic stress disorder prevalence among former first responders: A systematic review and meta-analysis

A meta-analysis on a population most of the literature ignores. Active personnel get the studies; the retired and medically separated walk into the consult later, often without language for what they carry. This is the figure beneath the post-career arc Waystone coaches against.

Systematic review and meta-analysis of PTSD prevalence among former first responders, gathering studies on retired, medically separated, and otherwise post-career fire, EMS, and law enforcement personnel. The authors aggregate prevalence estimates, examine heterogeneity across study design and screening instruments, and name the methodological gaps that keep this population undercounted.

Try this: Pull the article into intake reading. The post-career arc is its own clinical-adjacent terrain and deserves its own framing in the Waystone offer.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

America's Moral Injury Pandemic

Time names moral injury at population scale. Litz and Shay's frame, scaled up and pushed into popular circulation. Worth reading for the language clients will arrive holding, even where the simplifications grate. Useful as a reference when a brother first encounters the term and needs to know he is not alone in it.

Long-form piece arguing moral injury is no longer a niche military concept but a structural feature of contemporary American life. Surveys clinicians, veterans, and healthcare workers shaped by institutional betrayal. Treats moral injury as a public health frame rather than a personal pathology, leaning closer to the Litz and Shay structural-critique camp than the operational performance-psych frame.

Colorado partners with MAPS to deliver free Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders statewide. This is the program template Phoenix Fire and the AZ Department of Health should be watching. Worth flagging up the chain at CAP and through Waystone networks.
