Posttraumatic stress disorder prevalence among former first responders: A systematic review and meta-analysis

Foundation data on the population a first responder coaching practice actually serves. Most PTSD literature pools active and former responders together; this work pulls former apart cleanly. Useful for naming what does not retire when the uniform comes off.

Systematic review and meta-analysis of PTSD prevalence among former first responders, including retired or separated firefighters, paramedics, police, and dispatchers. Aggregates existing literature into a defensible population estimate. The signal beneath the numbers: PTSD often surfaces clean only after the schedule, the crew, and the role have fallen away, right when peer support structures also fall away.

Try this: For intake with retired or recently separated responders, screen explicitly for the post-career symptom emergence pattern, not just current symptoms.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

MAPS and State of Colorado Partner to Deliver Free Harm Reduction Training for First Responders

This sits at the exact intersection of Mark's two worlds: state-sanctioned, free, aimed at the responders who answer the calls when someone is mid-experience and flooded. Responder-facing harm reduction rather than advocacy press.

MAPS announces a partnership with Colorado's Natural Medicine Division to deliver the Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training free to first responders across the state. Includes video modules, assessments, and evidence-based de-escalation practice. Targets the operational gap between Colorado's regulated psychedelic program and the responders catching difficult experiences in the field.

Try this: Track whether Arizona behavioral programs adopt similar curriculum as state psychedelic policy matures. Worth a note to the behavioral side at Phoenix Fire.

State of the Science: MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for the treatment of posttraumatic stress disorder

A measured assessment in a journal that is not in advocacy posture. Required reading given the regulatory and credibility stress around MDMA-PTSD work after the Lykos rejection. State of the science papers lag the field by roughly a year, so read it as ground truth, not headline.

Formal scientific status paper on MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for PTSD, published in Volume 39, Issue 2. Covers efficacy data, limits of existing trials (blinding, expectancy, therapist effects), and the question of whether the evidence base supports the standard of care positioning the field has pitched. Written as scientific consensus, not advocacy.

Try this: Pull the PDF. If you are speaking, teaching, or holding integration sessions referencing MDMA work, this is the citation that keeps the language honest.

The physiological and affective mechanisms of shame among veterans with posttraumatic stress disorder symptoms

Shame is the backstage emotion in moral injury. This brings physiological measurement to it, the polyvagal-adjacent move: a felt state with a measurable substrate. Useful for the clients who cannot say much beyond reporting that the chest collapses when a particular memory comes near.

Examines the autonomic and affective signatures of shame in veterans with PTSD symptoms. Operationalizes shame beyond self-report, giving an objective marker. Findings inform whether shame-focused work needs somatic components rather than cognitive reframing alone.

Try this: When a client names guilt and the body reads as shame, trust the body. Slow the pace; do not push narrative.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

Active-duty US soldiers to receive MDMA therapy for PTSD next year

Federal-scale move that will reshape what civilian responders ask for and what insurance carriers eventually consider. Active-duty military is the test population the field has been waiting on. Implementation under DoD will differ from what private programs deliver, and that difference will matter.

Reports active-duty US soldiers will begin receiving MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD in 2027 through a defined DoD program. Frames the rollout as a federal acknowledgment that ceiling effects in existing PTSD treatments are severe enough to warrant the move. Open questions: which protocol, how providers are trained, what counts as treatment failure to qualify.

Try this: Watch for the published protocol. A civilian first responder analog will likely follow within two to three years if outcomes hold up under DoD scrutiny.
Source: The Guardian

No. 07 · Peer Support · 2026-05-07

Dena Ali: How leaders shape firefighter wellness

Ali argues firefighter wellness is shaped first by the conditions leaders create. Toxic command climates corrode wellness faster than any program can rebuild it; supportive leadership multiplies the effect of every wellness investment. Aimed at chiefs and company officers, with culture named as a leadership responsibility rather than a bumper sticker.

Wellness programs cannot outpace the culture set by those in command.

Dena Ali is one of the few names in fire service wellness who consistently puts leadership failure on the page as a driver of bad outcomes, rather than placing the load entirely on the individual responder. This sits in the moral injury and structural critique camp, not the resilience-as-individual-virtue camp.

Try this: For coaching engagements with chiefs or training officers, this is the right framing to introduce. Resilience downstream of structure, not parallel to it.
Source: FireRescue1

Former police officers left waiting for promised mental health support

Different jurisdiction, same pattern: promised support, missing delivery. The shape Mark sees domestically with different paperwork. Public conversation has caught up to the policy gap, and the gap remains.

Reports former police officers in Australia were promised mental health supports through their compensation and discharge process and have been left in a queue, some for more than a year. Names the gap between rhetoric and operational reality, and the risk that suicidality and substance use compound during the wait. A structural lag responders in any country recognize on first read.

Try this: When taking on a former responder client, screen whether their official support pathway has actually delivered. If not, that gap is part of the work.
Source: The Examiner
