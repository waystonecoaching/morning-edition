Posttraumatic stress disorder prevalence among former first responders: A systematic review and meta-analysis

Peer-reviewed, current, on Mark's exact population. The 'former' qualifier is what makes this useful. Most responder PTSD prevalence work samples active-duty crews; this one looks at what comes after the badge. Useful baseline number when describing risk to a client or to a department, in place of the unsourced figures that get repeated by reflex.

A systematic review and meta-analysis pooling prevalence studies of PTSD in retired or otherwise separated first responders. EarlyView publication in JTS, the field's primary peer-reviewed venue for trauma research. The contribution is the post-career frame, which is where most of Mark's coaching population sits.

Try this: Pull the prevalence figure from this paper before the next intake conversation. Replace whatever older number is in your client onboarding deck.

Active-duty US soldiers to receive MDMA therapy for PTSD next year

Department of Defense moves a small cohort of active-duty soldiers into MDMA-assisted therapy in 2027. Distinct from the FDA approval lane; this is a parallel federal track through military medicine, post-Lykos. Population is military, not first responders, so this is precedent rather than direct application. Worth tracking because the operational data will be cited at responders next.

The article reports DoD will begin offering MDMA-assisted therapy to active-duty service members with PTSD in the coming year, the most significant federal move on psychedelic-assisted therapy since FDA's 2024 rejection of the Lykos NDA. The pathway is an investigational protocol within the military health system rather than a civilian standard of care shift.

Source: The Guardian

The Room 16 Protocol: A Protocol for Supporting First Responders in Mental Health Crisis

Field-tested handoff protocol for a first responder presenting in active mental health crisis at a hospital. Closest cousin to what Phoenix Fire's behavioral crisis specialists already build into their work. Worth comparing against your current handoff procedure section by section. Note the scope-of-practice edge: this is acute crisis territory, not coaching territory.

The piece outlines a dedicated room and a defined protocol for first responders presenting at an emergency department in mental health crisis, designed to interrupt institutional re-traumatization. Components include separation from the public ER, peer support call-out, uniform handling, and confidentiality protocols. Built by First HELP and Safe Call Now operators familiar with the gaps in standard ER intake for uniformed personnel.

Try this: Cross-reference Phoenix Fire's existing crisis handoff against the Room 16 elements: peer call-out timing, confidentiality boundary, separation from public intake, uniform protocol.
Source: JEMS / First HELP

State of the Science: MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for the treatment of posttraumatic stress disorder

Peer-reviewed survey of where MDMA-assisted psychotherapy actually sits in the evidence base, written for the trauma research community rather than the advocacy press. The kind of paper Mark wants on hand when a BTTY referral asks whether MDMA is really a treatment yet. Read it for what is claimed, what is contested, and what is still being run on extrapolation post-Lykos.

A state-of-the-science review covering trial design, efficacy signals, methodological critiques, and post-Lykos regulatory posture for MDMA-assisted psychotherapy in PTSD. JTS, Volume 39, Issue 2, peer-reviewed. Sits alongside the FDA Adcomm reasoning rather than around it.

No. 05 · Resilience & Moral Injury · 2026-05-08

When your workplace doesn't match your ethical outlook: the problem of moral injury

Sits in the structural-critique camp of moral injury (Litz, Shay): the wound is produced by the institution, not by the individual call. Worth holding alongside the operational-resilience literature because the two lineages are in active tension and the tension is the content. When this gets flattened into 'build resilience' it loses its bite.

A short essay arguing that moral injury is often produced not by combat or by single horrific events but by chronic mismatch between an organization's stated values and the work it actually demands of its people. Names the structural source, distinguishes it from PTSD, and pushes back against framings that locate the injury inside the worker.

Source: The Conversation

MAPS and the State of Colorado partner to deliver free harm reduction training for first responders

MAPS partnering with Colorado's regulated psychedelic program to deliver free harm-reduction training to police, fire, and EMS for handling psychedelic-related field calls. Adjacent to BTTY's lane in lineage and substance, distinct in posture (state-regulated harm reduction, not depth integration). Worth knowing about if a Phoenix paramedic asks where this curriculum lives.

MAPS announced a partnership with Colorado's Natural Medicine Division to offer Psychedelic Harm Reduction and Integration training to first responders at no cost. Curriculum covers field assessment of someone in a difficult psychedelic experience, de-escalation without restraint as the default, and integration referral pathways. Funded through Colorado's regulated psychedelic program.

Try this: If anyone at Phoenix Fire asks how to handle a psychedelic-related call without defaulting to physical restraint and benzodiazepines, this is the curriculum to point them at.

Sisyphus: The Purpose Is in the Push

A reflective piece arguing that the repetitive thanklessness of firefighting (the same calls, the same systems, the same losses) is itself the source of meaning, not an obstacle to it. Pulls Camus's Sisyphus into the firehouse and reads it for camaraderie, adaptability, and acceptance of what cannot be fixed. Operational-resilience tradition; does not engage the structural-injury frame.

Operational-resilience camp, not the structural critique. The frame is Camus by way of the kitchen table: the stone is the work, the meaning is in the pushing. Useful, with a caveat. This register can drift into the stoicism-and-suffering pattern that hides moral injury rather than naming it. Read the piece, then read it against the moral injury essay above.

Dena Ali: How leaders shape firefighter wellness

Dena Ali (Raleigh FD, founder of First Responder Mental Health) carries field-level credibility on firefighter wellness, distinct from program-vendor language. Leadership-shaped culture is the lane Mark works adjacent to via the behavioral crisis specialist role. Listen for what she names that the wellness press tends to miss.

An interview-format piece on the role of company-officer and chief-officer leadership in shaping whether firefighters under pressure ask for help or hide it. The argument is that programs do not save line firefighters; lieutenants and captains, by what they tolerate and how they respond, do. Posture before infrastructure.

Coaching is human-centered. Can AI help?

ICF guidance on AI tools in coaching practice. Practical for a solo practitioner thinking about workflow without crossing the empathy-substitution line. The boundary work is what Mark already does manually around scope-of-practice questions; this gives the industry framing.

ICF makes the case that AI cannot replace the relational empathy at the center of coaching but can support administrative and reflective workflow. Industry-level framing rather than vendor pitch.
