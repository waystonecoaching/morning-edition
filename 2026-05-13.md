**Link:** [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70073?af=R](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70073?af=R)

**Why this matters to you:** Most PTSD prevalence work in the first responder space studies active personnel. This one studies the people who have left. That distinction matters for retired Phoenix Fire members, for medics who walked away, for officers who finished a career carrying something they never named. The post-career window is exactly where coaching, integration, and pastoral care are most needed and least staffed.

**The story:** Meta-analysis pooling prevalence estimates for PTSD among former firefighters, police, paramedics, and dispatchers. The pooled estimate runs meaningfully higher than active-duty comparisons in several subgroups, with heterogeneity tied to time since separation, exposure type, and measurement instrument. The authors call for longitudinal follow-up beyond retirement rather than cross-sectional snapshots.

**Link:** [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70075?af=R](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70075?af=R)

**Why this matters to you:** Shame is the affect that hides longest in the responder population and the one most likely to be misread as cynicism, withdrawal, or burnout. **The story:** Lab study examining heart rate variability, skin conductance, and self-report affect during shame induction in veterans with PTSD symptoms versus controls. The discussion connects to existing work on social-engagement system disruption.
