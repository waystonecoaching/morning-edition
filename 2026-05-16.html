MAPS and Colorado Partner on Free Psychedelic Crisis Training for First Responders

This is the cleanest current example of psychedelic-aware response training reaching the people who actually run the calls. Worth tracking as Colorado data accumulates, because Arizona and other states will reference it when their own decriminalization debates land in front of fire and EMS leadership.

MAPS and the Colorado Department of Revenue's Natural Medicine Division are providing the Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention curriculum to first responders statewide at no cost. The program includes evidence-based education, de-escalation practice, and assessment tools aimed at calls involving psychedelic use.

Try this: Pull the MAPS curriculum overview and compare it against the de-escalation framework you currently use on behavioral crisis runs. Note any gaps where existing protocols default to medical clearance plus restraint when a calmer, present-with approach would serve.
Source: MAPS

Executive Order Fast-Tracks Psychedelic Treatment for Veterans

Federal pressure is now moving faster than the evidence base. Read this as policy momentum that will create access, not as a science-led endorsement. First responders share enough of the veteran trauma profile that whatever rolls out for VA care will shape what your clients ask about within twelve months.

An April executive order directs the VA and partner agencies to accelerate psychedelic-assisted therapy access for veterans, with downstream implications for how schedules, prescriber training, and reimbursement get built out.

Psilocybin for Chronic Suicidal Ideation: Aaronson on Safety and Expectations

Most psychedelic trials have excluded the patients you and BTTY would consider hardest. Aaronson is one of the few investigators running studies with chronic suicidality as the inclusion criterion. Scope-of-practice edge: this is where coaching, integration support, and treatment of active suicidality have to be held in distinct hands. Worth reading slowly.

Aaronson reports a real safety signal and a meaningful efficacy signal in patients with chronic suicidal ideation receiving psilocybin, while flagging expectation effects as a serious confound. The piece is candid about what the data does and does not yet support.

Source: Psychedelic Alpha

State of the Science: MDMA-Assisted Psychotherapy for PTSD

After the FDA advisory committee rejected the Lykos application, the literature is recalibrating. This review is the kind of measured reassessment the field needs more of. If clients ask why MDMA therapy is suddenly harder to access, this is the citation to anchor the answer.

A peer-reviewed summary of where MDMA-PTSD research actually stands, including the methodological criticisms that drove the FDA decision and what remains promising despite them. Useful as a reset of expectations after several years of forward-leaning press coverage.

Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

PTSD Prevalence Among Former First Responders: Meta-Analysis

Most prevalence data focuses on active responders. This systematic review looks at what happens after the badge comes off, which is the population your Waystone work actually serves most often. A meta-analysis pooling studies on PTSD prevalence in former first responders. Strong methodology, direct relevance to the post-career population that finds you through the network rather than through the department.

On What 'The Brain Keeps the Score' Actually Means

A new paper revisits what is and is not supported by the popular claim that trauma is stored in the body. The article works through the actual mechanisms, separating the rhetorical from the neurophysiological.

Felt safety is the precondition. Story comes later, if at all.

A useful corrective to the way van der Kolk's title has been used as a slogan. The autonomic story Deb Dana teaches is more precise than 'the body keeps the score,' and conflating the two has muddied client expectations. Worth re-reading from the polyvagal frame you already work in.

Source: Psychology Today

Minneapolis Firefighters Taking PTSD Leave at Increasing Rates

About thirty firefighters out of one department on PTSD leave is a small enough number to dismiss and a large enough number to break a staffing model. The piece frames it as a budget problem, which is the door the union and the chief will both walk through before the wellness conversation happens. Read it as a window into where the conversation is now allowed to start in fire service leadership.

Roughly thirty Minneapolis firefighters are out on PTSD leave, straining staffing and driving up overtime costs. The article tracks the financial pressure and the policy questions that follow when a duty-related mental health claim becomes a routine line item.

Source: Firefighter Nation

Spectator Violence as a Form of Moral Injury

Moral injury keeps stretching past its original wartime frame, and the literature is the better for it. This piece works the seam between witness and complicity, which is the same seam a paramedic walks every shift at the cardiac arrest where the family freezes in the doorway. A useful close for the morning: not a takeaway, an invitation to sit with what we have all seen and not yet metabolized.

A theological essay arguing that being a spectator to violence carries a moral wound distinct from being its agent or its victim. Useful framing for the bystander stories your clients sometimes carry without naming them as trauma.

Source: The Christian Century
