MAPS and Colorado Partner on Free Psychedelic Crisis Training for First Responders

This sits exactly where Mark's two worlds meet: Phoenix Fire behavioral crisis work and the BTTY psychedelic integration lane. Colorado is now state-funding the harm-reduction curriculum first responders will actually encounter on calls. Whatever direction Arizona drifts, this is the template other states will look at.

MAPS announced a partnership with Colorado's Natural Medicine Division to deliver free Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders across the state. The curriculum covers evidence-based education, de-escalation practices, and field-level scenarios for encounters with people under the influence of psychedelics. Materials include videos and assessments. This is the first state-level deployment of the program at scale.

Try this: Worth pulling the curriculum outline and seeing whether it can be referenced inside Waystone client work or shared with the Phoenix BCMHS team.

PTSD Prevalence Among Former First Responders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

This is Mark's exact coaching demographic measured at scale: retired and separated responders carrying PTSD past the badge. Numbers from a meta-analysis sit differently in conversation than a single-study figure. Worth knowing when describing the field to a referrer or a prospective client.

A peer-reviewed systematic review and meta-analysis in the Journal of Traumatic Stress pulls together prevalence estimates for PTSD among former first responders. Until now the bulk of published work has tracked active-duty personnel; former responders have been the harder cohort to study because they leave the structures that capture their data. The paper synthesizes the available evidence base for that population.

Try this: Pull the full text before the next intake call with a retired responder. Use the prevalence figure if a client minimizes their experience.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

Psilocybin for Chronic Suicidal Ideation: Aaronson on Safety, an Efficacy Signal, and the Role of Expectations

Scope-of-practice edge. Most psilocybin trials exclude high-acuity patients; Aaronson's group at Sheppard Pratt has made them the focus. This is the population Mark sees on the Phoenix calls and never on the coaching roster. Worth tracking because it shapes what referral partners on the psychiatric side will and will not consider, and it raises the question of where coaches sit when a client is moving toward this kind of treatment.

Psychedelic Alpha interviews Scott Aaronson on his Sheppard Pratt group's psilocybin research with chronically suicidal patients. The conversation covers safety profile in high-risk samples, an early efficacy signal, and the methodological problem of expectancy effects in psychedelic trials. Aaronson distinguishes between what the data can support and what advocacy is overstating.

Source: Psychedelic Alpha

Texas Marks Moral Injury Awareness Day to Support Veterans and First Responders

Awareness-day pieces are usually thin, but the structural framing matters. Moral injury sits in the Litz and Shay lineage as a wound of betrayal and conscience, not a syndrome of fear-conditioning. Naming it on a state calendar gives clinicians and peer support teams language they were not handed before.

Texas designated May 14 as Moral Injury Awareness Day, recognizing the wound carried by veterans and first responders whose service has crossed lines they cannot reconcile. The release frames moral injury as distinct from PTSD and points to organizations developing screening, peer support, and clinical pathways. It positions the day as a public marker rather than a clinical initiative.

Source: PR Newswire via Moral Injury Project

Short-Term and Late-Term Effects of Psilocybin in Major Depression: A Randomized Clinical Trial

JAMA, not advocacy press. The field is under credibility stress, and the durability question matters more than the acute effect: short-term response is well-established, late-term response is where the case either holds or collapses. Worth reading with the methodology in view, not the headline.

A randomized clinical trial published in JAMA reports both short-term and late-term effects of psilocybin on major depressive symptoms. The paper extends beyond the typical six-week window into follow-up assessment, which is where most prior trials have not had power to speak. Results are presented as effect sizes at multiple timepoints rather than a single endpoint.

Try this: Read the methods section before the framing.
Source: JAMA

No. 07 · Psychedelic Therapy · 2025-11-11

Facing Trauma Under the Influence of Psychedelics: Nova Rave Survivors

A mixed-methods phenomenological study in the Journal of Psychopharmacology examines trauma response in survivors of the October 7, 2023 Nova rave attack, the majority of whom were under the influence of psychedelics during the event. The authors document how the substance state shaped encoding, memory, dissociation, and later integration. Findings complicate the simple story that psychedelics buffer or amplify trauma in any uniform direction.

The substance state shaped not the trauma itself, but how it was laid down and how it returned.

A natural experiment no IRB would ever approve: a mass-casualty attack in which most of the people present were under the influence of psychedelics. The phenomenology of trauma encoded in that state is what every careful integration practitioner has wondered about. This piece sits on the edge of what coaching can hold and what therapy must hold; the framing in client work matters.

Source: Journal of Psychopharmacology

A New Paper Says the Brain Keeps the Score. Here's What That Actually Means.

The phrase has been so abused in trauma-influencer culture that it is worth seeing what the paper itself actually claims. Mark's framework holds the body as substrate, not metaphor; sloppy popularization is what makes it hard to teach the real thing to clients who have already read the cliff notes.

Psychology Today walks through a recent paper unpacking what the original van der Kolk thesis empirically supports and where the popular version has drifted. Useful for coaches and clinicians who teach this material and want to keep the science separate from the slogan.

I Saved a Cat Once: A Retired Fire Chief Deals With Humor and Trauma in Memoir

Closing on a human-scale piece. A retired chief writing toward what he carried, in the firehouse vernacular Mark knows. Humor is one of the older ways the work gets metabolized, and one of the ways it gets hidden. The memoir tradition is itself a form of integration; the Desert Fathers were not the only ones who wrote things down so they would stop carrying them alone.

Firefighter Nation profiles a retired Washington fire chief whose memoir uses dark humor and direct prose to write through a career of accumulated trauma. The piece sits with how he holds the material without rushing past it, and how the writing functioned as its own form of integration. Recommended as a model for responders who do not want a clinical frame but need somewhere for the material to go.

Source: Firefighter Nation
