From therapeutic promise to evidentiary discipline: Reassessing MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for posttraumatic stress disorder

The credibility problems in MDMA-assisted therapy research are not going away. This JTS paper takes a rigorous look at what the evidence actually supports versus what the field has been claiming -- methodological issues, unblinding, expectancy effects. Anyone doing psychedelic integration work needs to hold this critically.

A peer-reviewed reassessment published in the Journal of Traumatic Stress argues that the MDMA-PTSD research base needs stricter evidentiary standards before the field can claim what it has been claiming. The piece examines therapeutic alliance confounds, unblinding in trials, and the gap between Phase 2 outcomes and Phase 3 replication. It does not dismiss the therapy outright but calls for methodological discipline the field has not consistently applied.

Try this: If you have clients who ask about MDMA-assisted therapy, this is the piece that lets you hold both the promise and the honest uncertainty in the same hand.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

Reconciling hippocampal semantic representations of traumatic memory in PTSD

This is the neuroscience underneath why trauma memory behaves the way it does -- fragmented, context-stripped, non-linear. Understanding the hippocampal encoding failure helps explain what integration is actually doing at a mechanistic level.

A paper in Neuropsychopharmacology examines how traumatic memories are encoded differently in the hippocampus compared to ordinary autobiographical memory. In PTSD, the semantic coherence of the memory is disrupted -- the contextual and temporal scaffolding that normally frames an event gets stripped away, leaving a fragmentary sensory record that fires without narrative containment. The paper proposes a model for how therapies that restore semantic representation might reduce intrusion symptoms.

Source: Neuropsychopharmacology (Nature)

Sourcing Challenges for Ibogaine May Slow US Veterans' Psychedelic Therapy Push

Ibogaine has produced striking results in veteran PTSD and TBI contexts -- the Stanford data was compelling. The supply chain problem is real and worth understanding before telling clients or partners this is a near-term option.

Despite growing evidence that ibogaine may be effective for veterans with PTSD and TBI, sourcing the compound is becoming a significant obstacle. Ibogaine is derived from the African iboga plant, and international supply chains are limited, inconsistent, and entangled in legal and conservation concerns. Efforts to synthesize ibogaine or use analogues are in early stages. The piece notes this could significantly slow the momentum building around veteran psychedelic therapy programs.

Source: Hemp Gazette

MAPS and State of Colorado Partner to Deliver Free Harm Reduction Training for First Responders

Since Colorado legalized natural medicines, first responders are encountering psychedelic-related calls without training for them. MAPS is offering free crisis assessment and intervention training. This is the practical interface between the psychedelic therapy world and fire and EMS culture -- and it is coming whether departments are ready or not.

MAPS and Colorado's Natural Medicine Division have partnered to provide free Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders across the state. The training covers how to recognize and respond to adverse psychedelic experiences without escalating them into psychiatric emergencies or criminal matters. Colorado is the first state to build this kind of systematic first responder capacity around natural medicine legalization.

Try this: If you are doing any work with Phoenix Fire around mental health or partner development, this Colorado model is worth filing. Arizona's legal landscape is different but the training gap is identical.

Posttraumatic stress disorder prevalence among former first responders: A systematic review and meta-analysis

This is the citation you have needed. A systematic review with pooled prevalence data for PTSD in former first responders -- the numbers that have been quoted loosely in the field now have a clean source.

A systematic review and meta-analysis published in the Journal of Traumatic Stress synthesizes PTSD prevalence data across studies of former first responders -- those post-service or post-career. The analysis addresses methodological heterogeneity across studies and provides pooled estimates with confidence intervals. The findings are relevant to understanding what the cumulative load of a career in fire, EMS, or law enforcement actually produces over a lifespan, not just during active service.

Your Vagus Nerve Is Not in Control: The Problem with Polyvagal Theory

Mad in America is not a neutral source and this piece has an agenda, but the scientific critique is worth engaging. The core argument is that polyvagal theory overextends what the anatomy and physiology actually support -- the myelinated vagus does not work the way the standard model implies. Know the pushback if you are grounding client work in this framework.

The piece argues that polyvagal theory, as popularized in trauma and therapy contexts, makes claims about vagal anatomy and physiology that exceed the current evidence. The critique focuses on the idea that the ventral vagal complex operates as a distinct social engagement system in the clean hierarchical way the theory describes. The author draws on neuroscience literature to question the mechanism without necessarily dismissing the clinical observations that motivated the framework. This is not new criticism -- Stephen Porges has responded to versions of it -- but it is reaching a wider audience.

Source: Mad In America

Human Coaching Boosts Adherence in AI Wearable Obesity Study

The argument that human coaching adds irreplaceable value over automated systems keeps getting empirical support. In a wearable-based weight loss intervention, human coaching significantly outperformed the AI-only condition on adherence. The relational container is not decoration.

A study reported in Medscape found that participants in an AI wearable-based obesity intervention who also received human coaching showed significantly better adherence than those receiving the wearable and automated feedback alone. The effect was attributed primarily to accountability, relational attunement, and the ability of the human coach to adjust framing based on what was actually happening for the client. The AI system was more consistent; the human coach was more effective.

Try this: Useful framing for conversations with potential clients or referral partners who ask whether coaching adds value beyond apps and trackers.
Source: Medscape

How understanding moral injury could save veterans' lives

Moral injury is not PTSD. The mechanism is different -- it is not about fear but about the fracture of conscience, the wound that opens when what a person did, witnessed, or failed to prevent violates what they hold sacred. Getting that distinction right changes how you work with it.

The DAV piece makes the case for moral injury as a distinct construct from PTSD, with different roots, different presentation, and different pathways toward healing. The article reviews current understanding of moral injury in veterans and advocates for screening and treatment approaches that address the moral and spiritual dimensions rather than treating it as a subtype of trauma. The piece surfaces the risk that misclassifying moral injury as PTSD leaves the actual wound untouched.

Source: DAV (Disabled American Veterans)
