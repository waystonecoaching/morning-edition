Veterans' Positive Parenting Practices and Satisfaction over Time: Examining the Impact of Moral Injury, Posttraumatic Stress, and Internalizing Symptoms

Moral injury and PTSD don't stay at the station. This JTS study tracks how these symptoms shape parenting behavior and satisfaction over time in veterans, which is the same cluster Mark sees in first responders: the withdrawal or overcorrection with kids, the way operational stress reshapes what family feels like. High-credibility peer-reviewed research. Relevant for understanding the full life context a coaching client brings in.

Longitudinal study examining how moral injury, posttraumatic stress, and internalizing symptoms affect parenting practices and satisfaction in veterans over time. Finds that symptom levels shape parenting quality across the measurement window, with implications for understanding the domestic consequences of occupational trauma. Peer-reviewed, Journal of Traumatic Stress.

Try this: Useful framing for intake conversations with clients who flag family tension without connecting it to their work history.

Posttraumatic Stress Disorder Prevalence among Former First Responders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

The 'former' designation here matters: this is what happens after the career ends, when the structure is gone and the nervous system is still running at operational tempo. From Therapeutic Promise to Evidentiary Discipline: Reassessing MDMA-Assisted Psychotherapy for Posttraumatic Stress Disorder

This is the piece to read alongside any MDMA trial announcement. JTS is asking whether therapeutic claims for MDMA-assisted psychotherapy have outpaced the evidence. The field is under credibility stress post-Lykos, and this reassessment names what rigorous review actually requires. Scope note: this is clinical research critique, not coaching material. For anyone doing integration work, where the evidence actually stands shapes how you can honestly frame what clients ask about.

Peer-reviewed reassessment of MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for PTSD in the Journal of Traumatic Stress. Examines methodological standards, critiques what evidentiary discipline requires, and presses the field to close the gap between therapeutic promise and confirmable data. Published the same month the VA launched its own MDMA trial.

MAPS and State of Colorado Partner to Deliver Free Harm Reduction Training for First Responders

A different frame than the clinical therapy pipeline. Harm reduction training for first responders positions them as responders to psychedelic crisis, not candidates for therapy. That role distinction is honest and useful. MAPS building this training pipeline matters as community-level psychedelic use expands and departments need language and protocol for what they encounter in the field.

MAPS partnered with Colorado's Natural Medicine Division to offer free Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders statewide. The program teaches de-escalation, evidence-based education, and field intervention for psychedelic emergencies. Training is delivered via video and assessment modules.

Try this: Colorado model worth watching for Arizona replication. Contact MAPS about whether the program extends to other states or whether Phoenix Fire behavioral health could pilot the training.
Source: MAPS

Investigating the Safety and Tolerability of Single-Dose Psilocybin for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder: A Nonrandomized Open-Label Clinical Trial

Early-phase, open-label, no control group. Effect sizes carry accordingly. This is among the first trials to investigate single-dose psilocybin specifically in a PTSD population. The psilocybin research lane has moved more carefully than the MDMA lane, and the early data here is preliminary but from a credible journal. Scope note: clinical research outside coaching scope.

Open-label trial testing single-dose psilocybin in participants with PTSD. Reports preliminary evidence of tolerability with no serious adverse events at the dose studied. Small N, no randomization; this is early signal, not confirmatory. Published in Journal of Psychopharmacology, January 2026 issue.

Source: Journal of Psychopharmacology

Your Vagus Nerve Is Not in Control: The Problem with Polyvagal Theory

Mad In America is a critical-of-psychiatry outlet; read accordingly. That said, the polyvagal critique is real and deserves engagement. The neuroscience questions about PVT have been raised by researchers outside that platform too: Porges's anatomical claims have been challenged, and the clinical extrapolations often outrun the data. Mark uses polyvagal as a framework for clients, not a scientific claim. That distinction matters when explaining it to skeptics or deciding how hard to lean on the mechanism versus the clinical utility.

A critique arguing that the vagus nerve does not function in the hierarchical, sequential way Porges describes, and that pop-psychology applications of PVT have advanced well beyond what neurobiological evidence supports. Published in Mad In America, a platform that consistently challenges mainstream psychiatric frameworks.

Try this: Worth reading to sharpen how you explain polyvagal to clients: frame it as a useful map of autonomic patterns, not a settled neuroscientific account.
Source: Mad In America

How Understanding Moral Injury Could Save Veterans' Lives

The DAV writing about moral injury signals that veteran service organizations are catching up to what the research has been naming for a decade. This is the institutional translation layer: the concept is moving from academic literature into the organizations that actually touch veterans and, by extension, first responders. Useful context for how Mark explains moral injury to clients who have never heard the term.

DAV piece on how understanding moral injury, distinct from PTSD, can change how veterans are identified and supported. Covers the core distinction between trauma as injury from outside threat and moral injury as damage to one's sense of right action. Frames it through the lens of veteran outcomes and suicide risk.

The concept of logismoi in the Evagrian tradition, intrusive thoughts born from violated conscience, runs through the entire hesychast stream. This piece will not use that language, but it is doing the same work. Worth the read as a reminder that the tradition Mark stands in has always held this.

A Patheos piece engaging with moral injury from a spiritual and religious perspective, examining how faith communities understand and respond to the experience of having violated one's deepest sense of right action. Explores the concept outside the clinical frame.
