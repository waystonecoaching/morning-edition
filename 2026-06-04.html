MAPS & State of Colorado Partner to Deliver Free Harm Reduction Training for First Responders

Colorado is the first state with legal natural medicine access, and this program is the first systematic attempt to prepare first responders for psychedelic-adjacent field encounters. MAPS offering its Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention curriculum to responders is the clearest current intersection of the two worlds Mark works in. This is operational training, not therapy-provider training; it prepares first responders to de-escalate rather than escalate when they encounter someone in a psychedelic crisis. Worth knowing as a training reference and as a template for Arizona as legalization conversations develop.

MAPS announced a partnership with the Colorado Department of Revenue's Natural Medicine Division to provide free harm reduction and psychedelic crisis assessment training to first responders statewide. The curriculum covers evidence-based education, de-escalation practices, and crisis intervention for field encounters with individuals under the influence of natural medicines. The program is free, video-based with assessments, and developed specifically for the first responder context rather than adapted from clinical training materials.

Try this: If Mark is developing educational programming for Phoenix Fire around behavioral crisis response, the MAPS Colorado curriculum is a live model to cite and potentially adapt. Colorado's natural medicine framework is roughly eighteen months ahead of where Arizona regulatory conversation currently sits.

Your Vagus Nerve Is Not in Control: The Problem with Polyvagal Theory

Source flag first: Mad in America has an explicit anti-psychiatry editorial line that colors how it frames even technically grounded arguments. The underlying neuroscience critique of polyvagal theory predates this piece and has peer-reviewed footing; Grossman and Taylor's 2007 anatomical analysis is the reference worth reading directly. The practical distinction for Mark's practice: the critique targets the mechanistic claim about vagal anatomy and the three-tier hierarchy, not the clinical utility of autonomic state awareness. Mark teaches the polyvagal ladder to clients; knowing the specific limits of the model lets him hold it accurately when a skeptical clinician or informed client asks.

Mad in America piece challenging the mechanistic claims of polyvagal theory, focused on whether vagal anatomy and function actually support the three-tier social engagement hierarchy that Porges and Dana popularized. The neuroscience critique holds that the myelinated ventral vagal pathway plays a more limited role in mammalian social behavior than the model implies, and that clinical and popular framing has outrun the neurobiological evidence. The critique does not invalidate autonomic state work with clients; it narrows the claims that can be made with confidence about the specific mechanism.

Try this: Read Grossman and Taylor (2007) directly rather than relying on this secondary framing. Know the anatomy limits before the question comes up from a clinician collaborator or a well-read client.

Multiscale cardiorespiratory complexity reveals autonomic signatures of Rajyoga meditation

Published today in Frontiers. Rajyoga is not hesychasm; the contemplative mechanics are different. But the question the study asks, whether trained stillness produces measurable autonomic signatures, is one the Desert Fathers would recognize. What they called nepsis, watchfulness, sobriety of attention, is not a metaphor for HRV coherence; it is prior to it. The data is preliminary. The question is ancient. That they converge is worth sitting with.

Frontiers study of 55 trained Rajyoga practitioners using multiscale HRV metrics and HRV-respiration coupling to characterize autonomic state changes across pre-meditation, during-meditation, and post-meditation phases. The study finds within-subject state-dependent changes in cardiorespiratory complexity, suggesting that trained meditation produces distinguishable autonomic signatures. Sex-stratified analysis is exploratory. The mechanism being traced, attention and stillness producing measurable physiological coherence, is the same substrate underlying polyvagal-informed somatic work.

Source: Frontiers in Psychology
