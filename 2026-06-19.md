**Source:** Journal of Traumatic Stress  \
**Category:** first_responder_mh  \
**Published:** 2026-06-04  \
**Link:** [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70086?af=R](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70086?af=R)

**Why this matters to you:** The first thing a rigorous systematic review does is show you how much the field does not know. Paramedic PTSD prevalence estimates scatter widely because the studies used different tools on different samples, and the protective factor research is thin. That gap is the operating environment for a first responder coaching practice. Knowing where the evidence ends is as useful as knowing where it begins.

**The story:** A Journal of Traumatic Stress systematic review synthesizes the available global literature on PTSD prevalence, risk factors, and protective factors in paramedic populations. Prevalence estimates range widely due to heterogeneous assessment instruments and sampling methods, making a single reliable number elusive. Risk factors include cumulative traumatic exposure, poor peer and organizational support, help-seeking stigma, and the absence of structured post-incident processes. Protective factors identified include social connection, psychological flexibility, and access to culturally competent mental health resources. The review notes that the gap between paramedic exposure levels and available mental health infrastructure remains significant and largely unaddressed in the literature.
