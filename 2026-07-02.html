Is moral injury claiming our healthcare leaders?

Moral injury in leadership is the upstream version of what Mark sees downstream in first responders. When the people running systems can't hold the systems accountable, the injury propagates. Worth reading for how the authors frame the distinction between burnout and moral injury.

Health Services Daily examines whether moral injury is reaching into healthcare leadership, not just frontline workers. The piece looks at organizational betrayal as the mechanism: when leaders are forced to implement policies that violate their own values, the wound is the same as in field personnel. The framing is clinical-adjacent but the pattern is identical to what surfaces in first responder coaching work.

'One very valuable tool': Retired Fla. firefighters launch peer support effort around bench

Peer support built by people who've been in the seat. The bench format is a low-barrier entry point worth knowing about as a model.

Retired Florida firefighters launched a peer support initiative centered on a physical gathering bench at their station, creating a standing informal space for check-ins. The simplicity of the model is its strength: it normalizes conversation without requiring a formal program designation or clinical referral. Grassroots, low-cost, and replicable.

MAPS & State of Colorado Partner to Deliver Free Harm Reduction Training for First Responders

MAPS training first responders in psychedelic crisis assessment and intervention is a direct intersection of Mark's two worlds. This is the infrastructure piece, not the therapy piece: how do departments handle encounters with people mid-experience. Relevant to any conversation about psychedelic-informed coaching with first responder clients.

MAPS and the Colorado Department of Revenue's Natural Medicine Division are jointly delivering free Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders statewide. The program covers evidence-based de-escalation and harm reduction for encounters with people experiencing psychedelic effects. The training is video-based with assessments, designed for broad department reach rather than specialist depth.

Try this: If Phoenix Fire or AZ agencies don't have equivalent training, this is worth flagging to department contacts. Colorado's model is replicable.

Directly applies

Eight weeks of guided slow breathing alters stress responses in veterans

Nervous system regulation via breath is the polyvagal case study here. Eight weeks is a short intervention with measurable autonomic effect. Relevant for how Mark frames breath-based regulation work with first responder clients without straying into clinical protocol.

A study reported by PsyPost found that eight weeks of structured slow breathing practice produced measurable changes in stress response patterns in veterans, including shifts in heart rate variability and self-reported reactivity. The researchers used guided sessions rather than self-directed practice. The findings suggest that consistent breathwork within a contained time frame is sufficient to alter baseline autonomic function.

Try this: Eight weeks is a plausible coaching container. This is a concrete framing for clients who want to understand why breath practice matters physiologically.
Source: PsyPost

Compassion Fatigue Confronting Moral Injury: Healing Clinicians

Compass fatigue and moral injury are often conflated. When they're distinguished clearly, it opens a more specific path for the person in the chair. The soul cost of healing others is the question that doesn't retire when the shift ends. This is the edition's close because it is the question underneath most of what came before it.

Pharmacy Practice News addresses the distinction between compassion fatigue and moral injury in clinicians, noting that conflating them leads to interventions that miss. Compassion fatigue is depletion; moral injury is wound. The piece examines what healing looks like for people whose identity is bound up in giving care, and how the system that produces the wound is rarely equipped to tend it.

Source: Pharmacy Practice News
