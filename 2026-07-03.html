Neuroplastogen Treatment for Posttraumatic Stress Disorder

Neuroplastogens are a distinct pharmacological class from classic psychedelics, designed to produce neuroplasticity effects without full psychedelic experience. JAMA covering this in the PTSD context means the field is widening beyond MDMA and psilocybin. Worth understanding the mechanistic distinction before clients start asking about it.

A JAMA piece on neuroplastogen treatment for PTSD examines compounds designed to promote neuroplasticity without the full subjective psychedelic experience. The category includes agents like MDMA analogs and novel compounds that target plasticity pathways independently. This frames a possible third lane alongside traditional psychedelics and current pharmacotherapy. The piece addresses both mechanism and early clinical evidence. Read alongside the executive order piece for context on where the field is actually heading versus where the headlines are.

Behavioral health in the workplace: Supporting fire fighter well-being

IAFF hosted a webinar titled 'Psychological Safety, Workplace Incivility, and Institutional Betrayal: Organizational Factors Shaping Mental Health in First Responders.' The frame shifts focus from individual resilience deficits to organizational factors that create or worsen mental health injury. Institutional betrayal, in the research literature, refers specifically to the harm caused when an institution fails to prevent or respond adequately to wrongdoing against its own members. In fire culture, this can mean inadequate peer support structures, failure to act on reported harassment, and the gap between public messaging about mental health and actual departmental behavior.

Institutional betrayal is the organizing concept here. When the organization a firefighter has given their career to fails to protect or actively harms them, the wound is different from operational trauma. It fractures the relational container that makes other coping possible. This is showing up in Mark's client work and the IAFF is finally naming it directly.

MAPS & State of Colorado Partner to Deliver Free Harm Reduction Training for First Responders

First responders are now encountering people in psychedelic states on calls. Colorado is training them to de-escalate rather than escalate. If Arizona follows, Phoenix Fire will want this. Worth knowing the training exists and where to point people.

MAPS and the Colorado Department of Revenue's Natural Medicine Division are partnering to deliver free Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders statewide. The curriculum covers evidence-based education on psychedelic states, de-escalation practices, and intervention protocols designed to reduce harm when responders encounter someone in a psychedelic experience. The program is video-based with assessments. It is free and available to any Colorado first responder.

Try this: Model program worth flagging to Phoenix Fire behavioral health leadership. The MAPS training URL is at maps.org and the Colorado program structure could be a template for Arizona.

Eight weeks of guided slow breathing alters stress responses in veterans

Polyvagal theory predicts that paced breathing changes autonomic state by influencing vagal tone. Eight weeks of guided slow breathing producing measurable changes in stress responses in veterans is the theory operationalized into a dosing question. How much practice, at what frequency, produces what change? This study gives some numbers.

PsyPost reports on research showing that eight weeks of guided slow breathing practice altered physiological stress responses in veterans. The study measured changes in heart rate variability, cortisol markers, or related autonomic indicators. The finding aligns with polyvagal predictions about respiratory influence on vagal tone and ventral vagal access. The intervention is low-cost, portable, and applicable outside clinical settings. This is not the hesychast tradition's account of why breath matters, but it is the measurable substrate of what that tradition describes.

Try this: The eight-week duration is a useful number for client conversations about somatic regulation practices. Not a quick fix, not a lifetime commitment. Eight weeks is a realistic trial frame.

PSIP Literature Review: Relocating the Root

The psychedelic therapy field has built its legitimacy case primarily on veterans, first responders, and survivors of sexual violence. This piece asks what happens when the care model that was designed around those populations gets extended to people impacted by the criminal legal system. The equity question underneath it is real and the clinical frameworks may not translate without modification.

MAPS' Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy for Incarcerated and Justice-Involved Persons literature review examines how psychedelic care models developed for veterans and first responders may need to be restructured to serve people who have been directly impacted by the War on Drugs. The piece identifies ways that existing frameworks carry assumptions about therapeutic alliance, institutional trust, and the meaning of integration that do not transfer cleanly. It is a critical literature review rather than an advocacy piece, which makes it more useful. The contemplative traditions that underlie much of the integration framework also emerge from desert and marginal spaces, not from positions of institutional safety.

Source: MAPS
