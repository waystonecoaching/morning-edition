**Why this matters to you:** Small study, but it lands exactly on the autonomic substrate claim: the intervention changed sympathetic reactivity under stress, not resting state. That distinction matters when describing to clients what breath practice actually does. It does not sedate the system; it changes how the system meets load.

**The story:** PsyPost covers a randomized trial of 25 veterans with PTSD assigned to eight weeks of daily device-guided slow breathing or a sham device. Resting heart rate, blood pressure, and resting sympathetic nerve activity were unchanged. Under a mental stress test, though, the breathing group's sympathetic nerve activity spiked far less than at baseline, and the effect held even when the device was not in use. Published in the American Journal of Physiology. The sample is small; read it as converging evidence, not proof.

**Actionable:** Clean client-facing language: the practice trains the stress response, not the resting state. Pairs well with the polyvagal framing already in use.
