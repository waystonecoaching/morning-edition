Psychedelics produce enduring enhancement of reward responsiveness in male rats

Mechanism, sober and preclinical. Before the trial press and the policy fights, this is the layer where the claims are made or broken. Enduring change in reward responsiveness speaks to anhedonia, the flat, joyless deadness that trails PTSD and depression. Rats are not people, and a durable behavioral signal in a rodent model is a hypothesis, not a promise.

A Neuropsychopharmacology study reports that a single psychedelic exposure produced lasting increases in reward responsiveness in male rats. The finding points at a possible mechanism for the antidepressant and anti-anhedonic effects seen in human trials, namely a durable shift in how the brain registers reward. It is primary preclinical literature, valuable precisely because it stays close to the biology rather than the marketing.

Source: Neuropsychopharmacology (Nature)

How Motivational Interviewing Supports Neurodivergent People - Psychology Today

Motivational interviewing and your non-directive lineage share a spine: the person holds their own knowing, and the practitioner's job is to make room for it rather than steer. This piece on neurodivergent clients is a useful stress test of that stance, where autonomy and clear structure both have to be honored at once. Getting Real would call it staying in contact without taking the wheel.

Psychology Today argues that motivational interviewing suits neurodivergent people because it centers autonomy, reduces pressure, and works with a person's own reasons for change rather than imposing an agenda. It contrasts this with directive approaches that can misfire. The relevance is methodological: it echoes the non-directive inquiry at the center of a Being True to You practice.
