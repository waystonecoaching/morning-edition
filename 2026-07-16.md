**Link:** [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70089?af=R](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70089?af=R)

**Why this matters to you:** This is the distinction Mark works inside every week. Moral injury and PTSD share a surface, but the DSM-5-TR now carries a Z-code for moral problems, which gives clinicians and coaches a shared vocabulary for the difference between a fear-conditioned nervous system and a violated conscience. The sharper the boundary, the cleaner the referral decision.

**The story:** The paper argues that moral injury is not a subtype of PTSD but a distinct construct rooted in the transgression of moral belief, whether by one's own action, another's, or betrayal by leadership. The authors examine how the DSM-5-TR moral problems Z-code lets clinicians name moral suffering without medicalizing it as a disorder. They map where the two conditions overlap in symptom presentation and where they diverge in mechanism and repair. The practical upshot is that fear-extinction protocols aimed at PTSD may miss what moral injury actually needs.

**Actionable:** When a client's pain reads as guilt, shame, or betrayal rather than threat, note the moral-injury frame in your session record and check whether the work belongs with a chaplain or clinician.
