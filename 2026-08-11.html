MAPS Responds to Report of Progress on MDMA-Assisted Therapy Resubmission to FDA

This is the headline everyone in the psychedelic-PTSD space will be reading today. After the 2024 rejection, the renamed sponsor (formerly Lykos, before that MAPS PBC) has resubmitted its new drug application for MDMA-assisted therapy. For your work this matters twice over: it moves the standard-of-care question for responder and veteran PTSD, and it sits squarely on the scope line, because if this clears, integration coaching around a legal, prescribed protocol becomes a live question rather than a hypothetical.

MAPS issued a statement responding to a Psychedelic Alpha report that Resilient Pharmaceuticals has resubmitted its NDA for MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD to the FDA. The resubmission follows the agency's 2024 complete response letter, which cited trial design and data-integrity concerns rather than a rejection of the underlying signal. MAPS frames the move as progress while keeping distance from the for-profit sponsor it spun off. The read here is measured optimism from an organization with reputational skin in the game.

New Assessment Protocol Makes Moral Injury Easier to Identify in Those at Risk

Structural-critique lineage here, not the performance-psych one. A validated instrument that catches moral injury earlier is useful precisely because moral injury hides behind the same presentation as PTSD and gets treated as PTSD. For a coach working the pre-clinical space, an assessment vocabulary helps you name what you are seeing and hand off cleanly when the picture crosses your line.

Researchers at the University of Bath report a new assessment protocol designed to identify individuals at elevated risk of moral injury before it entrenches. The work positions moral injury as distinct from post-traumatic stress, driven by transgression and betrayal rather than fear. Earlier identification is the stated aim, with the implicit argument that current tools miss it. Treat this as instrument development worth watching, not yet a settled screening standard.

No. 05 · Psychedelic Therapy · 2026-08-07

Psilocybin Therapy Sessions for PTSD Are Mostly Silent, Study Finds

This is the quiet, useful one. A close look at what actually happens inside a dosing session finds mostly silence, which cuts against the assumption that the therapist is steering. It lands near your BTTY training directly: non-directive presence, the client's own inner movement, the discipline of not intervening. The hesychast will recognize the posture. Attention held, mouth closed.

A study examined the verbal content of psilocybin-assisted therapy sessions for PTSD and found that most of the session time is silent, with therapist talk concentrated at the edges rather than during peak effects. The finding challenges the idea that clinical outcomes come from active in-session processing. It supports a model where the therapist holds a safe container and the participant does the interior work. Sample sizes in this space remain small, so read it as a texture-of-practice finding rather than a mechanism claim.

Source: PsyPost

Israeli Study Reports PTSD Symptoms Eased in 80% of Soldiers With MDMA Therapy

Read the number, then read it critically. An 80% response figure from an open-label or small cohort in a wartime population is a headline, not a controlled result. It matters because it feeds public expectation ahead of the FDA question in story one, and expectation is exactly what erodes credibility when a larger trial regresses to the mean. Hold the hope and the skepticism in the same hand.

Coverage of an Israeli study reports that roughly 80% of soldiers receiving MDMA-assisted therapy saw PTSD symptoms ease. The population is combat-exposed, which makes the signal relevant to responder work but also carries selection and expectancy effects. As reported through news outlets rather than a peer-reviewed primary source, the design details that would let you weigh it are thin. The responsible posture is interest without endorsement.

The federal counterweight to the MDMA story. This is the pipeline to watch over the MDMA headline, because a VA-run psilocybin arm is harder to dismiss and slower to hype.

The VA is launching a large study of psilocybin for veterans with depression and PTSD, reported as a significant government-run entry into psychedelic research. Federal sponsorship raises the evidentiary bar and lends the eventual results more durability than industry or advocacy trials. The scale and design details will determine how much it moves practice. For now it signals that the psilocybin question has institutional legitimacy, whatever the outcome.

Recovering From Moral Injury by Breaking the Silence

Close on this. The claim is old and true: moral injury metabolizes in confession, in being heard without being fixed. The Desert Fathers knew it as the disclosure of thoughts to an elder, silence broken not for advice but for witness. Every framework on your shelf points to the same door. Contact is the medicine. Presence before technique.

The article argues that recovery from moral injury begins when the person breaks the silence around what they did or failed to do and lets it be witnessed. It frames concealment as the mechanism that keeps the wound active and disclosure as the turn toward repair. The register is human-scale rather than clinical, which is its strength. It ends where the contemplative tradition begins: shame loses its grip when spoken into a safe relationship.
