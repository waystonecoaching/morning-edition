Two Years After Rejection, Resilient Quietly Refiles MDMA for PTSD Application

If this NDA clears, the standard of care for responder PTSD shifts under your feet within a year of approval. Clients will arrive having already read the headline. The word quietly is doing work here: the sponsor rebranded twice since the 2024 rejection and is not running a press campaign this time, which reads as a company that learned something about scrutiny.

Resilient Pharmaceuticals, formerly MAPS PBC and then Lykos Therapeutics, has resubmitted its new drug application for MDMA-assisted therapy in PTSD. The FDA rejected the original filing in August 2024 and asked for an additional Phase 3 trial, citing functional unblinding and concerns about how therapy was delivered and documented alongside the drug. Psychedelic Alpha reports the refiling happened without a public announcement. MAPS issued a separate statement acknowledging the report. The company has not published the data package supporting the resubmission.

Try this: Draft the two-sentence answer you will give when a client asks whether MDMA therapy is available yet. Keep it factual, keep it short, and route treatment questions to a prescriber.

Moral Injury in the Texas First Responder Workforce: Findings and Policy Recommendations

This is the structural-critique lineage, not the resilience-training lineage. It locates the wound in policy, staffing, and command decisions rather than in the responder's coping capacity. Arizona is not Texas, but the mechanisms travel: mandatory overtime, thin behavioral health coverage, and workers comp regimes that reward silence.

A policy report examining moral injury across the Texas first responder workforce, with findings framed for legislators rather than clinicians. The argument treats moral injury as an occupational and organizational outcome, produced by conditions the employer controls, and recommends policy changes at the department and state level. Right On Crime is a criminal justice policy organization, so the frame is reform-oriented and the audience is lawmakers.

Try this: Note which recommendations have an Arizona analogue. Useful language if a department ever asks you to speak to leadership rather than to line crews.
Source: Right On Crime

Allegheny County (PA) 911 Dispatchers Are Confronting a Mental Health Crisis. Their Own.

Dispatchers are the forgotten tier. They carry the audio and none of the resolution: the call ends and they never learn whether the patient lived. That unresolved arc is a dorsal setup, and it compounds across a shift in a way scene work does not. This piece involves completed and attempted suicide, so it sits on the high-acuity edge. If a dispatcher lands in your practice, screen for current risk and hold a referral path open before you do any integration work.

Reporting on the mental health situation among Allegheny County 911 dispatchers following a dispatcher suicide in 2024 and an attempted suicide in June. Staffing pressure, mandatory overtime, and the absence of dispatcher-specific behavioral health support are named as contributing conditions. Dispatchers are frequently excluded from the presumptive coverage and peer support structures that field responders receive, despite comparable cumulative exposure.

Try this: If your intake language says fire, EMS, and law enforcement, add telecommunicators. They rarely see themselves in the copy and rarely ask.
Source: Firefighter Nation

Major VA Study to Examine Effectiveness of Psilocybin on Depression, PTSD

The VA is the largest integrated health system in the country and it moves the field by moving reimbursement and training pipelines, not by publishing one trial. Multiple sites are standing up, including Tuscaloosa and others reported this week. Veterans and first responders overlap heavily in your referral stream, so what the VA normalizes reaches your intake within a couple of years.

The Department of Veterans Affairs has launched a multi-site clinical trial of psilocybin-assisted therapy for veterans with depression and PTSD. It follows earlier federally funded work on MDMA and represents the agency moving from pilot posture to a larger coordinated study. Design details, comparator, and blinding approach are the elements worth watching, since functional unblinding was the specific problem that sank the MDMA application.

Source: Military Times

Enhancing Cognitive Processing Therapy via Stellate Ganglion Blocks: A Pilot Randomized Trial

This is autonomic intervention in its most literal form: an anesthetic block of the cervical sympathetic chain, delivered so that talk therapy can land afterward. It is a direct empirical test of the claim your framework rests on, that sympathetic tone gates access to reprocessing. If it holds, felt safety is not a metaphor about the nervous system. It is a dosing question. Note the scope line clearly: SGB is a procedure, CPT is therapy, and neither is coaching.

A pilot randomized trial testing whether stellate ganglion block improves outcomes when added to cognitive processing therapy for PTSD. The rationale is that reducing sympathetic activation lowers the physiological floor enough for cognitive work to take hold. Pilot trials are powered for feasibility and signal, not for effect size you can quote to a client, so read this as a direction rather than a finding.

Try this: Worth having a plain sentence ready for clients who come back from an SGB asking what to do with the window it opens.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

New Assessment Protocol Makes Moral Injury Easier to Identify

Screening instruments migrate. A protocol built for research use shows up eighteen months later in a wellness program, and then in a coach's intake packet, and by then nobody remembers it was never validated for that. Read this for the construct, not for the form. Administering a moral injury assessment and interpreting a score is assessment, which is over the clinical line.

Researchers report a new assessment protocol intended to identify individuals at elevated risk for moral injury more reliably than existing measures. Moral injury has resisted measurement because it overlaps with PTSD and depression while being distinct from both: the core is a violation of moral belief rather than a threat to life. Better identification is the precondition for both treatment research and policy work.

Try this: Read the construct definition and check it against how you describe moral injury to clients. Do not adopt the instrument.
Source: Medical Xpress

Moral Injury Doesn't Always Look Like Moral Injury: What Leaders Need to Recognize

A field-level argument that moral injury frequently presents as something else: cynicism, withdrawal from the crew, abrupt disciplinary problems, or a decision to leave the job that gets read as burnout. The piece is written for military leaders and argues that the recognizable presentations are the late ones. Earlier signs cluster around a person quietly withdrawing consent from the mission rather than around visible distress.

The recognizable presentations are the late ones. What comes first looks like a good operator going quiet.

Set this against the Texas policy report. Same wound, opposite lever. This piece asks leaders to recognize and respond; the policy report asks legislators to change the conditions that produce it. The operational frame is faster and lands inside existing command structures. It also risks making moral injury a leadership skills problem when the injury often originated in a leadership decision. Both are true. Hold the tension.

MAPS and Colorado Partner on Free Psychedelic Crisis Training for First Responders

This is the exact intersection of your two roles. Behavioral crisis response meets psychedelic harm reduction, delivered free, with a state regulator as partner. Colorado built it because regulated psilocybin services created a call volume nobody had trained for. Arizona has no equivalent yet, which is the opening. Older item, still the most directly usable thing in today's edition.

MAPS partnered with the Colorado Department of Revenue's Natural Medicine Division to deliver its Psychedelic Crisis Assessment and Intervention training to first responders at no cost. The training covers de-escalation and harm reduction for people in acute psychedelic distress, an encounter type that standard crisis intervention curricula do not address. The partnership follows Colorado's regulated natural medicine rollout.

Try this: Look at the curriculum outline this week. If it is portable, it is a concrete thing to bring to Phoenix Fire behavioral health and a credential that separates Waystone from every other responder coach in the state.

Psilocybin Therapy Sessions for PTSD Are Mostly Silent, Study Finds

Empirical confirmation of what every sitter already knows and almost nobody writes down. The work in the room is presence, not intervention. This also cuts at the regulatory question: if the session is mostly silence, what exactly is the therapy component the FDA is being asked to approve, and who is qualified to hold it. That question is unresolved and it determines the training lane you and every other BTTY-trained practitioner end up in.

Researchers coded audio from psilocybin dosing sessions in PTSD trials and found the large majority of session time contains no speech. Verbal exchange clustered at the beginning and end, with the dosing peak passing in silence. The finding complicates how psychedelic-assisted therapy is described in protocols, where the therapeutic content is often specified as though it were continuous.

Source: PsyPost

Recovering From Moral Injury by Breaking the Silence

The oldest technology in the file. A wound carried alone stays a wound; spoken to someone who does not flinch, it becomes something a person can put down. The Desert Fathers called it exagoreusis, the disclosure of thoughts to an elder, and they were explicit that the point was not absolution but the breaking of concealment itself. Speaking is the whole intervention.

An accessible piece arguing that silence is the mechanism that keeps moral injury in place, and that recovery begins with disclosure to someone capable of receiving it without judgment. Shame maintains the concealment, and concealment maintains the shame. The article notes that the listener matters as much as the telling: disclosure to someone who reacts badly can deepen the injury.

Try this: One question for the next session that needs it: what part of this have you not said out loud to anyone.
Source: Psychology Today
