Two Years After Rejection, Resilient Quietly Refiles MDMA for PTSD Application

If this clears, MDMA-assisted therapy becomes a standard-of-care question for responder PTSD inside your working lifetime, and integration referrals stop being hypothetical. Read the resubmission strategy carefully. No new Phase 3 means the 2024 Complete Response Letter concerns about durability, safety characterization, and functional unblinding are being answered with reanalysis and argument rather than fresh data. That is a legitimate regulatory path and also the exact move that draws scrutiny. Hold both.

Resilient Pharmaceuticals, formerly MAPS PBC and then Lykos Therapeutics, has resubmitted its new drug application for MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD. The FDA rejected the original filing on August 9, 2024, citing durability of response, incomplete safety characterization, and steps needed to minimize bias. The resubmission advances without a new Phase 3 trial. Proponents argue core efficacy and adverse event reporting held up despite partial functional unblinding. FDA issued final guidance in July 2026 codifying methodological expectations for psychedelic investigations, including therapist-intervention standardization and long-term follow-up, which raises the bar this application has to clear.

Try this: Draft your one-paragraph answer for the client who asks whether MDMA therapy is available yet. Name the filing, name the unresolved bias question, and name the referral you would actually make today.

Pa. 911 dispatchers seek expanded support amid mental health crisis

Dispatchers carry the call without the closure of the scene. They hear the worst of it, hand it off, and take the next one, and the classification question is not bureaucratic trivia. It gates their access to presumptive coverage and peer support. This one sits on the acuity edge. Two suicide events at a single call center is a cluster, not an anecdote, and cluster response is a clinical and command problem before it is a coaching one.

Allegheny County dispatchers have pushed for years for mental health support equal to what field responders receive. A dispatcher died by suicide at the Moon Township 911 center in August 2024, and another operator attempted suicide in the same parking lot in June. Union leadership says the recent attempt shows how much is still undone. Advocates are pursuing legislation to reclassify emergency dispatchers as first responders, which would open benefits currently closed to them. At the state level, the County Commissioners Association of Pennsylvania noted the 2026-27 budget again omitted funding increases for 911 centers and county mental health services, though it did fund administrative support for warm handoffs to crisis services.

Try this: If your peer support outreach map stops at the apparatus floor, extend it to the comm center this week. Dispatchers are almost never on the list.

N.M. behavioral health team expands 911 response with paramedics, field medication

This is your job description with a different agency patch on the sleeve, and it is the clearest picture yet of where the co-responder model is heading. Field administration of an antipsychotic by a paramedic embedded in a behavioral unit moves the team across a real line, from de-escalation and navigation into chemical stabilization. Worth watching what that does to trust on repeat contacts. The Santa Fe contrast in the same reporting is the warning: these units do not fail loudly, they thin out.

Bernalillo County's Behavioral Health Unit pairs licensed clinicians, sheriff's detectives, and county fire paramedics on behavioral health calls. The unit has grown to 19 staff since standing up in summer 2023 and took more than 3,000 911 calls last year. In January, paramedics assigned to the unit received authorization to administer sublingual olanzapine in the field to stabilize people in mania or psychosis, whether from a mental health condition or drug-induced. Onset runs about 20 minutes. Santa Fe's Alternative Response Unit, launched in 2021, has moved the other direction, losing staff and call volume.

Try this: Pull the Bernalillo staffing ratio, five clinicians and four case managers to three paramedics, and set it beside your own unit's. The case management depth is what keeps the model from becoming a transport service.

Operator Syndrome and Moral Injury

Operator Syndrome is the closest thing the military side has to a name for what twenty years on a busy engine does to a body and a conscience at the same time. The argument here is whether moral injury belongs inside the syndrome or beside it, and that is not academic. If it is inside, it gets treated as one more system to manage. If it is beside, it stays what it is, a violation of conscience that answers to confession, restitution, and trust rather than to sleep hygiene and hormone panels. This piece belongs to the structural-critique lineage, not the performance-optimization one.

Chris Frueh coined Operator Syndrome in 2020 for a cluster clinicians kept seeing in special operations patients that fit no single diagnosis: traumatic brain injury tangled with endocrine dysfunction, chronic pain, sleep disruption, and existential distress. Frueh's original schematic left moral injury out. In 2024, clinical ethicist Michele DeMarco proposed folding in a moral and spiritual domain and built her own version of the model. The piece argues moral injury is categorically different from the somatic components, since it is a violation of conscience and its treatment runs through guilt, meaning, and trust. A validation study from the VA Palo Alto Intensive Evaluation and Treatment Program posted to medRxiv in November 2025 remains a preprint and is not peer reviewed.

Try this: In your next intake with a long-tenure responder, ask separately about sleep, pain, and conscience. Collapsing the third into the first two is the most common way moral injury gets missed.

Moral injury can be devastating: New assessment protocol makes it easier to identify those at risk

A validated instrument changes what you can say and what you must not. Screening tools tempt a coach across the line into assessment, and you are not licensed to diagnose moral injury. The value here is different: a validated measure gives you shared vocabulary with the clinician you refer to, and it gives the client language for something they have been carrying without a name. Read it for the construct definition, not for a form to hand out.

Psychologists at the University of Bath developed and validated a new measure for identifying moral injury, published in the European Journal of Trauma and Dissociation in July 2026. It is the first such instrument developed and validated specifically for UK populations. The tool is intended to help clinicians and researchers identify affected individuals, track symptom change over time, and evaluate treatment outcomes. The team frames moral injury as psychological, emotional, and social distress following events that violate a person's moral or ethical beliefs, and notes elevated risk of depression, anxiety, PTSD, suicidality, and alcohol misuse in that population.

Try this: Read the construct definition and check it against how you currently describe moral injury on the Waystone site. Where your language is looser than the measure's, tighten it.

80% of Soldiers in Israeli Study See PTSD Symptoms Ease With MDMA Therapy

The number is real and the denominator matters. Eighty percent of those who completed a thirteen-session protocol with two therapists in the room for eight hours at a stretch is not the same claim as eighty percent of soldiers with PTSD. Small sample, short follow-up, open-label, and the investigators say so themselves. Cite it as signal, not as proof, and notice what the protocol actually costs in clinician hours. That is the number that decides whether this ever reaches a fire department.

A study at Clalit Health Services' HaEmek Medical Center, run through its Center for Psychedelic Treatment and Research, evaluated MDMA-assisted psychotherapy in Israeli soldiers with PTSD. About 80 percent of soldiers who completed the protocol showed significant symptom reduction, and most no longer met diagnostic criteria. The protocol runs 13 sessions combining intensive psychotherapy with three medically supervised MDMA sessions of up to eight hours each, with two experienced therapists present throughout. Researchers ran the study amid a roughly 70 percent rise in PTSD diagnoses since October 7. They cautioned the sample was small, follow-up limited, and the treatment experimental. MDMA-assisted psychotherapy is not approved for clinical use in Israel or the United States.

Oregon congresswoman proposes program to support first responder mental health

Rep. Andrea Salinas, a Tigard Democrat representing Oregon's 6th District, announced a bill on August 17, 2026 at a Tualatin Valley Fire and Rescue station. It would create a grant program funding $7.5 million a year nationwide for suicide prevention, peer support, and counseling for firefighters and EMS staff. The bill also funds family services support and training to identify and respond to mental health crises within fire and EMS ranks. Cosponsors include Gabe Evans, R-Colorado, Sharice Davids, D-Kansas, and Tom Kean, R-New Jersey.

$7.5 million a year, nationwide, for suicide prevention and peer support. That is roughly two dollars per career firefighter and EMT in the country.

Bipartisan cosponsors on a first responder mental health bill is the part to watch, because it signals the issue has cleared the partisan filter. The funding level is the other part. Grant programs at this scale fund pilots and training cohorts, not sustained clinical capacity, which means the delivery burden lands on peer teams and outside practitioners. That is where a solo practice with responder fluency has a door. Watch for the grant language on qualified providers.

Try this: Track the bill number when it posts. If the grant language names peer support training as an allowable cost, that is a contracting lane worth watching.

PTSI, Psychedelics, and the Fire Service

The trade press is now covering psilocybin as a fire service topic rather than a fringe one, which changes the conversations you will have on the apparatus floor. Arizona is running a psilocybin trial for responders with hundreds of firefighters and officers enrolled, so this is local ground, not distant news. The scope line matters here and it is sharp. Integration coaching is not administration, not clinical care, and not a referral pipeline, and the moment a firefighter reads a piece like this and asks you how to get some, you need that sentence ready in your own words.

Firefighter Nation frames post-traumatic stress injury as an ongoing occupational threat to firefighters and argues psilocybin-assisted healing may be an option for some responders alongside standard treatments. The framing is additive rather than replacement: conventional evidence-based care remains the baseline and psychedelic-assisted work is presented as a possibility for a subset. Coverage in the fire trade press has shifted over the past two years from case reports of individual firefighters toward structured trials and state legislative activity.

Try this: Write your scope-of-practice sentence today, before someone asks. One sentence, said out loud, that names what integration work is and what it is not.

Grapevine (TX) Professional Firefighters Association Votes No Confidence in Chiefs

Read this through Shay and Litz rather than through labor relations. A no-confidence vote is what betrayal of what is right by someone in legitimate authority looks like when it finally gets a procedural form. Whether the chiefs are correct on the EMS shift is a separate question from what a firefighting force does with the belief that leadership will not protect its capacity to do the work. The autonomic reading is the same: chronic mobilization with no route to resolution, held collectively. That is the substrate the next critical incident lands on.

The Grapevine Professional Firefighters Association voted no confidence in the department's chiefs. Members cited low morale and poor communication from leadership. The central operational grievance is that the department's shift toward EMS is reducing fire response capability. The dispute reflects a wider pattern in departments where call volume has moved decisively toward medical while apparatus and staffing decisions are made on that basis.

Try this: When a client's presentation is organizational rather than incident-driven, name it as such early. Treating structural betrayal as a personal regulation problem is the fastest way to lose a first responder client.

Psilocybin therapy sessions for PTSD are mostly silent, study finds

The Fathers would recognize this finding. What the participants named as the active ingredient was not what was said but that someone stayed, awake and unhurried, without needing the hours to produce anything. That is a description of watchfulness, and it is closer to what you already practice than to what the word therapy usually implies. It also raises the harder question for anyone building a training lane: if the work is quiet presence, what exactly is being trained, and how would you know it was absent.

A study published in the Journal of Psychopharmacology analyzed what actually happens during high-dose psilocybin sessions for PTSD. Participants and support staff spend the large majority of session time in silence. Support relies on quiet, unobtrusive presence rather than active dialogue, and the model functions closer to passive monitoring than to conventional talk therapy. Interview data indicates that the quiet presence of staff was not experienced as absence. Participants described it as actively producing a strong sense of safety.

Try this: Sit one session this week without filling a silence you would normally fill. Notice what the client does with the room you left.
