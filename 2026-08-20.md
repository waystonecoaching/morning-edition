**Why this matters to you:** This is the first multisite look at what actually happens inside Oregon's regulated, non-clinical psilocybin market, published in JAMA Network Open. It matters because it describes the setting most people will encounter first, which is a licensed facilitator in a service center, not a research protocol. If you sit with clients on the integration side, this is the population you will meet. Note the scope-of-practice edge clearly: facilitators are not therapists, and four first-time users needed medical attention.

**The story:** Researchers followed 346 people who completed a single psilocybin session with a licensed facilitator at 24 Oregon service centers between November 2024 and June 2026. Participants reported at baseline, one week, one month, and three months. At one month, 91.5 percent said they benefited and 64.9 percent ranked the session among the ten most meaningful experiences of their lives. At three months, moderate to severe depression, anxiety, and PTSD symptoms had dropped, with improved wellbeing and life satisfaction. Harm reports were rare, though four first-time users had adverse behavioral reactions requiring medical attention.

**Actionable:** Read the JAMA Network Open paper itself before quoting the headline numbers. Self-selected, self-reported, single-arm, no control group. The effect is real enough to take seriously and soft enough that you should say so out loud to a client.

**The story:** A stellate ganglion block injects local anesthetic near the sympathetic ganglion in the neck, temporarily interrupting sympathetic outflow. **Actionable:** Add one line to how you describe autonomic state to clients: the nervous system is treatable at the level of the nervous system. You do not need to endorse SGB to make that point, and it lands differently than another breathing exercise.
