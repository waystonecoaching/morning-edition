**Why this matters to you:** If you describe the polyvagal frame to clients as autonomic state being the substrate of behavior, this paper proposes a mechanism for the part you already do without naming: that the practitioner's own state is an active ingredient, not a background condition. Hold it loosely. It is a hypotheses paper, which means it is a well-formed argument with no outcome data behind it, and the field is already crowded with polyvagal-adjacent theory that outran its evidence.

**The story:** The authors propose that autonomic dysregulation is a shared mechanism across anxiety, trauma-related, and mood presentations, with persistent sympathetic hyperarousal at one pole and dorsal shutdown or dissociation at the other. They build a model in which therapeutic change depends on autonomic synchrony between clinician and client rather than on content processing alone, drawing on the Swara tradition of breath and nasal-cycle regulation as an organizing frame. The paper states testable hypotheses and proposes measurement approaches. It reports no trial results.

**Actionable:** Read it for the hypotheses section, not the conclusions. If the synchrony claim holds, your own regulation before a session is a clinical variable and belongs in your pre-session practice rather than in your notes about the client.
