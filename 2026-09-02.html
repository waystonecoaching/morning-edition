Two Years After Rejection, Resilient Quietly Refiles MDMA for PTSD Application

The first rejection was never about whether MDMA works. It was about blinding failures, therapist conduct, and the integrity of the data package. A quiet refiling with no public accounting of what changed deserves a cold eye. For anyone doing integration work with responders, the practical question is whether the training and ethics infrastructure got repaired. Scope of practice edge: if approval lands, coaches will be asked to hold ground that belongs to clinicians.

Psychedelic Alpha reports that Resilient Pharmaceuticals, formerly Lykos Therapeutics and MAPS PBC, has resubmitted its new drug application for MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD. The FDA rejected the first filing two years ago over trial design and data credibility problems. The company made no announcement of its own. MAPS confirmed the report in a separate statement days later. What the resubmission contains remains undisclosed.

Try this: If a client asks about MDMA therapy timelines this month, tell them the application is refiled and nothing further is public. Resist forecasting.

Findings about resilience after trauma have been robust, yet mostly ignored

This sits in the applied and operational camp. Read it against the moral injury literature rather than in place of it. Both claims hold: most responders recover without treatment, and the ones who do not are often carrying a structural wound that resilience framing will never reach. The failure mode is using population statistics to wave off the man in the room.

The piece argues that decades of trauma research show most people exposed to potentially traumatic events never develop lasting disorder, and that the resilient trajectory is the statistical norm. Bonanno's trajectory work carries the argument. The author's complaint is that clinical training, research funding, and public messaging stay organized around pathology regardless of what the data show.

Bipartisan bill would fund firefighter, EMS mental health programs

Money follows legislation, and peer support programs in departments the size of Phoenix Fire run on scraps. Watch the grant language rather than the headline. A bill at introduction signals appetite and nothing more. The question that matters is whether funds would flow through state fire marshals or land directly with departments.

A bipartisan bill introduced in Congress would fund behavioral health programs for fire and EMS agencies, including peer support, counseling access, and suicide prevention. Coverage reports the introduction, not passage. No appropriation exists yet.

Try this: Capture the bill number once reporting names it, then check the funding pathway before anyone in the department starts planning against it.

Ohio city to route some 911 medical calls to nurses instead of EMS

Same architecture as behavioral crisis alternate response: pull low-acuity volume off the apparatus and route it to the right skill set. The politics run the same way too. Whether it works depends less on the triage protocol than on crew trust in the diversion and on whether the nurse line has anywhere to send people. Useful as a template argument when the behavioral side comes up for funding.

An Ohio city plans to divert low-acuity 911 medical calls to a nurse triage line rather than dispatching an ambulance. The stated goals are unit availability and fewer non-emergency transports. Implementation details and the acuity screen remain thin in the reporting.

Psilocybin reduces fear memory and restores neuroplasticity in the hippocampus and medial prefrontal cortex

Rodent work. It says nothing about what happens in a room with a man who carries a career of calls. What it offers is a mechanism story that maps onto the polyvagal claim that state precedes narrative: alter the substrate and the story becomes revisable. Treat it as a mechanism hypothesis and keep it out of client conversations until human data arrive.

Researchers report that psilocybin reduced conditioned fear memory and restored markers of neuroplasticity in the hippocampus and medial prefrontal cortex in an animal model of comorbid PTSD and depression. The authors attribute the effect to plasticity rather than to subjective experience. The design is preclinical throughout.

Source: Journal of Psychopharmacology

New Right On Crime Research Finds Moral Injury Affects Nearly Two-Thirds of Texas First Responders

This lands squarely in the structural critique camp, closer to Litz and Shay than to performance psychology. Moral injury and PTSD are different injuries, and the distinction carries the whole weight here. A responder who reads fine physiologically and still cannot square what he did with who he thought he was needs something other than a breathing protocol. Read the methodology before quoting the figure. Advocacy-adjacent research has a habit of measuring what it hoped to find.

Survey research released by Right On Crime reports that nearly two thirds of Texas first responders sampled show symptoms consistent with moral injury. The framing is policy oriented and aimed at state-level intervention. Instrument selection and sample construction are load bearing here and are not detailed in the coverage.

Try this: Pull the underlying instrument before this number goes into a client conversation or onto the Waystone site.
Source: Right On Crime

No. 09 · First Responder MH · 2026-09-02

Trauma-related intrusions moderate the persistence of negative affect in daily life: Evidence from ecological momentary assessment

Measurement catching up to what the autonomic frame already claims. The clinical unit is the recovery slope after a bad moment. That is Dana's territory, and it names what responders describe when they say the call ended hours ago and they still have not come back.

Using ecological momentary assessment, researchers found that trauma-related intrusions moderate how long negative affect persists across an ordinary day. Intrusions extend the tail of distress rather than only raising its peak. The design samples people inside their actual days instead of inside a lab or a clinic.

Try this: Stop asking a client how bad the week was. Ask how long it took to come back after the worst of it, and track that number across sessions.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

What "The Odyssey" Teaches Us About Moral Injury

The Desert Fathers would recognize the shape of it. A man can be inside his own house and still be at sea. What the poem holds, and what most protocols let slip, is that the work runs long and gets done in the company of others who will not flinch at what he says.

The essay reads Odysseus's long return as the portrait of a warrior who cannot come home, and treats the poem as a record of moral injury written down centuries before anyone named it. The argument is that the journey itself carries the healing. Coming home and arriving turn out to be two separate events.
