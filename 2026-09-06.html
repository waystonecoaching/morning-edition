Polyvagal Theory debunked in comprehensive new study

This is the one that touches your language directly. If you describe your work to clients or on the Waystone site in terms of ventral vagal safety and dorsal shutdown, you are now using vocabulary that a large body of physiologists has publicly called unsupported at the mechanism level. The critique is narrow and worth reading narrowly: Grossman evaluates the neurophysiology, not the clinical results. Deb Dana's interventions do not stop working because the myelinated vagus story is wrong about evolution and cardiac control.

Paul Grossman of University Hospital Basel led a paper co-signed by thirty-nine scientists, published in Clinical Neuropsychiatry under the title Why the Polyvagal Theory is Untenable. It argues that the core physiological claims of Porges's model, including the evolutionary account of the myelinated vagus and the specific mapping of autonomic states, do not hold against two decades of accumulated evidence. The paper addresses mechanism only and makes no claim about whether polyvagal-informed therapy helps people. Defenders, including a rebuttal in Psychology Today, argue the headline overreaches by treating a mechanistic critique as a clinical verdict.

Try this: Audit your own language this week. Where you say 'your dorsal vagal state,' try 'your body went still and far away.' The second sentence is truer, it survives the critique, and clients track it faster.
Source: InSight+ (Medical Journal of Australia)

New Zealand approves MDMA for PTSD treatment

Read the headlines against the regulator's own notice. Medsafe granted approval to two named psychiatrists, Gary Wynn in Wellington and Tom Paterson in Auckland, not to the profession. Every other prescriber has to apply on their own. The global press turned a two-clinician authorization into 'New Zealand approves MDMA,' which is how a field under credibility stress manufactures more of it. This is high-acuity clinical territory with a controlled substance, well outside coaching scope; your lane stays integration, and only alongside a prescribing clinician.

New Zealand's regulator authorized two psychiatrists to prescribe pharmaceutical-grade MDMA as part of a supervised treatment programme for adults with PTSD who have not responded to conventional care. Patients must be over eighteen and complete a comprehensive clinical assessment first. The move brings New Zealand into line with Australia, which has allowed authorised prescribers to deliver MDMA-assisted psychotherapy since 2023. Medsafe was explicit that the evidence base is still emerging and that authorisation remains limited to the two approved clinicians.

Pa+ Library: Psychedelic REMS

If you want to know what your integration work looks like in three years, read the REMS literature rather than the advocacy press. A Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy is where the FDA decides who may facilitate, in what room, with what training, and whether structured integration sessions are required or optional. That last question is the one that determines whether Being True to You practitioners sit inside the certified pathway or outside it. Track this the way you would track a protocol change at the department.

The piece surveys what a class-wide REMS for psychedelic therapies would likely require as approvals approach. Expected elements include certified prescribers who complete specific training, enrolled treatment sites meeting structural and staffing requirements, a trained facilitator present through roughly six hours of drug administration, and structured post-session integration. The FDA has pointed sponsors toward the Spravato REMS as a reference model, which implies a licensed provider on site and a prescriber available during treatment. Such a framework would look materially different from the state programs operating in Oregon and Colorado.

Try this: Ask your BTTY contacts one question this month: does the training lane you completed map onto a REMS facilitator certification, or would it need supplementing? Better to learn the answer now than after approval.

Attorney General Coleman launches peer support program for law enforcement

The design detail is the story. Kentucky's existing statewide capability responds to critical incidents, shootings and the like. These sub-grants deliberately fund the other half: consistent peer support for chronic stressors, the grinding accumulation that never generates a call-out. That distinction between acute and chronic is the same one you keep having to make when a department asks you to come do a debrief and then disappear. Money following the chronic side is a small structural win.

Kentucky's Attorney General opened a first-of-its-kind statewide grant program offering agencies up to five thousand dollars each to build or strengthen peer support teams. Allowable costs include overtime for peer support specialists, training and wellness education, and team equipment. The program is funded through the bipartisan Attorney General Alliance. It is framed explicitly as filling the gap left by incident-triggered response, targeting the chronic stress that accumulates between critical calls.

Source: Kentucky Association of Counties

No. 05 · First Responder MH · 2026-09-04

A cognitive model of moral and nonmoral self-criticism: the role of judgment criteria

Self-criticism is the water your clients swim in, and this paper does something useful with it: it separates moral self-criticism from the ordinary performance kind by looking at the criteria a person judges themselves against. That is not academic hairsplitting. The firefighter who says 'I should have gotten there faster' and the one who says 'I am the kind of man who lets people die' are running different machinery, and they need different work.

The authors review the major theoretical models of self-criticism and argue each captures a different slice of the phenomenon without accounting for its variety. They propose a cognitive framework centering on judgment criteria, the standards a person applies when evaluating the self, as the mechanism that explains why self-criticism takes such different forms across people. The model distinguishes moral self-criticism, where the criterion is a violated value, from nonmoral self-criticism, where the criterion is competence or performance. The paper is conceptual rather than empirical and offers a structure for future measurement work.

Try this: Next time a client runs themselves down, ask what standard they are measuring against before you touch the content. The criterion tells you whether you are in moral injury or in perfectionism.
Source: Frontiers in Psychology

Ohio city to route some 911 medical calls to nurses instead of EMS

Columbus is building the medical version of what you do on the behavioral side. Same premise: the ambulance is the wrong tool for most of what comes through the console, and the fix is a different responder rather than a faster one. Watch how they handle the handoff, because alternative response programs live or die on whether the caller feels dropped or transferred. That is where your role has always earned its keep.

Columbus began routing lower-acuity 911 medical calls to a nurse health care navigation line operated with a vendor partner. Callers still reach a dispatcher first, and if the call meets clinical criteria set by the Columbus Division of Fire, they are offered a direct connection to a nurse. City officials said more than half of the city's 2024 911 calls did not require an EMS response. In the soft launch that began in late July, 131 callers were diverted and more than half resolved their needs without an ambulance transport.

No. 07 · Resilience & Moral Injury · 2026-08-13

Operator Syndrome and Moral Injury

Operator Syndrome, coined in 2020 by a team led by Chris Frueh, names the cluster of physical, cognitive, and psychiatric problems that accumulate in special operations personnel from sustained high-tempo exposure and repeated head injury. Recent validation work in active-duty forces has evaluated moral injury as a clinically relevant domain within the construct. The SOF News commentary, written by a serving Navy chaplain, welcomes the addition and pushes further, arguing that treating a violation of conscience requires working with guilt, meaning, and trust rather than symptom load. It proposes that if Operator Syndrome becomes the vehicle for taking SOF-specific care seriously, spiritual care belongs inside the intake team rather than downstream of it.

A wound of conscience does not respond to a recovery protocol. It responds to being witnessed by someone who will not look away.

A Navy chaplain writing in a special operations outlet makes the argument you have been making in fire service rooms for years: if the clinical framework is finally taking hold, someone had better decide whether a chaplain seat belongs in the intake. This is the operational and the structural lineages colliding in public. Operator Syndrome is an allostatic-load model, built for performance recovery. Moral injury, in Shay's sense, is a wound of conscience and betrayal that no recovery protocol reaches. Adding one as a specifier to the other does not resolve the tension; it institutionalizes it.

How motivational interviewing can help with common mental health conditions

Worth reading as a boundary artifact rather than a technique piece. Motivational interviewing is the method that keeps migrating across the line between clinical care and coaching, and every time it does, the scope question gets fuzzier. Non-directive inquiry and MI share a spine, the refusal to argue someone into change, but MI carries a target the clinician holds and you do not. Knowing exactly where that differs is how you answer a referral source who asks what you actually do.

A clinical viewpoint written for general practice arguing that motivational interviewing has application beyond substance use into common mental health presentations. The core claim is that resistance is a product of the conversation rather than a property of the patient, and that eliciting a person's own reasons for change outperforms advice-giving in brief consultations. The piece is written for time-limited primary care encounters rather than extended therapeutic work.

Nolan's The Odyssey is an emotive meditation on modern moral injury

Shay built the modern concept of moral injury out of this poem, so the loop closing on a cinema screen has a certain rightness to it. The Desert Fathers would recognize the shape of the thing without needing the vocabulary: a man who has done what he cannot unmake, taking twenty years to get home, and finding that home is not the end of the work. Nostos is not healing. It is where the reckoning finally has a room to happen in.

Brian Powers, who directs the International Centre for Moral Injury at Durham, reads Nolan's film as a study in moral injury rather than PTSD. The distinction he draws is between a fear-conditioned disorder and damage to conscience, the harm done when a person perpetrates, witnesses, or fails to prevent acts that violate their own moral code. He notes that Shay derived the concept from this poem, defining moral injury as betrayal of what's right by a legitimate authority in a high-stakes situation. The commentary tracks how the film distributes that injury across Odysseus, Penelope, and Telemachus, and makes the audience a witness to it.

Source: Durham University
