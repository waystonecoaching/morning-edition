**Why this matters to you:** Self-criticism is the water your clients swim in, and this paper does something useful with it: it separates moral self-criticism from the ordinary performance kind by looking at the criteria a person judges themselves against. That is not academic hairsplitting. **The story:** The authors review the major theoretical models of self-criticism and argue each captures a different slice of the phenomenon without accounting for its variety. They propose a cognitive framework centering on judgment criteria, the standards a person applies when evaluating the self, as the mechanism that explains why self-criticism takes such different forms across people. The model distinguishes moral self-criticism, where the criterion is a violated value, from nonmoral self-criticism, where the criterion is competence or performance. The paper is conceptual rather than empirical and offers a structure for future measurement work.

**Actionable:** Next time a client runs themselves down, ask what standard they are measuring against before you touch the content. The criterion tells you whether you are in moral injury or in perfectionism.
