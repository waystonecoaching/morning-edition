### 01. Police work stress and occupational burnout: the chain mediating roles of work interference with family and affective work rumination

**Source:** Frontiers in Psychology  \
**Category:** first_responder_mh  \
**Published:** 2026-09-08  \
**Link:** [https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1955185](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1955185)

**Why this matters to you:** This is the mechanism question your clients live inside. Not whether the job is stressful, which nobody disputes, but how the stress travels from the shift into the house and then back into the body at 0200. The study names two carriers: work spilling into family life, and the emotional rumination that keeps the call running after the tones drop. Both are things a coach can work with directly, which is not true of the stressor itself.

**The story:** A cross-sectional survey of 1,464 police officers across nine cities in southeastern China tested whether the link between work stress and burnout runs partly through work interference with family and through affective work rumination. The authors used structural equation modeling to separate the direct association from three indirect paths. The design is cross-sectional, so the ordering is a model assumption rather than a demonstrated sequence. The sample is Chinese policing, which carries its own occupational and cultural structure, so treat the effect sizes as directional rather than transferable.

**Actionable:** Ask one client this week where the call goes after the shift ends. Not how they feel about it. Where it goes, physically and domestically.
