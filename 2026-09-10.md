**The story:** InSight+ covers the paper 'Why the Polyvagal Theory is Untenable,' published in Clinical Neuropsychiatry with 39 co-authors from autonomic nervous system research, led by Paul Grossman. **Actionable:** Draft two paragraphs of plain language you can say out loud when a client or a referral partner asks whether polyvagal is real. Separate the mechanism claim from the practice claim. Keep the practice claim tied to what you observe in the room, not to a diagram.

**Why this matters to you:** Closest population to yours in the current literature, and the trial design is honest about what it is. Twelve participants, no control arm, open label. **The story:** An open-label pilot trial published in Communications Medicine on 30 July 2026 tested psilocybin-assisted therapy in twelve veterans with severe, treatment-resistant PTSD. Participants received roughly eight hours of preparatory therapy, two dosing sessions at 15 mg and 25 mg spaced two to three weeks apart, and about eight hours of post-session therapy. No serious adverse events occurred and there was no increase in suicidal ideation or behavior, which was the primary safety question. PTSD symptoms fell by a mean of 27.5 points and 75 percent of participants were in remission one month after study end. The authors state plainly that uncontrolled pilots inflate effect sizes and that a randomized controlled trial is the necessary next step.

**Why this matters to you:** Twenty-five years on, the exposure is still being counted, and this study puts a structural number on something responders have described in vaguer terms for a decade: the memory going soft, the sharpness dulling. It also complicates the tidy separation between physical exposure and psychological injury. PTSD tracked with the aging signal independent of dust load. Worth sitting with on the eve of the anniversary rather than reading past.

**The story:** Researchers imaged World Trade Center responders with MRI and used a model trained on more than 11,500 scans from healthy people aged 5 to 95 to estimate brain age from structure. Responders with PTSD showed brains estimated roughly three years older than their chronological age. Responders without PTSD showed estimated brain age matching their actual age. Prolonged Ground Zero exposure was independently associated with the accelerated pattern. Around 23 percent of WTC responders carry a PTSD diagnosis, against a roughly 6 percent lifetime rate in the general population. The design is cross-sectional and cannot establish causation, but the association is consistent across the cohort.
