**Why this matters to you:** This is the one to read today. Thirty-nine scientists, led by Paul Grossman, put their names to a paper calling polyvagal theory untenable. It goes at the evolutionary claims, the clean ventral and dorsal split, and respiratory sinus arrhythmia as a proxy for vagal tone. Read the critique before you read anyone's rebuttal, and notice that it targets the physiology, not the clinical practice. Co-regulation still works in the room. The question is whether the story you tell a client about why it works is the right story.

**The story:** A multi-author review argues the neurophysiological and evolutionary foundations of polyvagal theory do not hold. Three lines of attack: the evolutionary account of a newer myelinated ventral vagus is weak, the anatomical separation between ventral and dorsal pathways is messier than the theory requires, and RSA is not a reliable index of vagal tone across conditions. Porges and defenders have answered in kind, and Psychology Today has already run a piece arguing the theory has not been debunked. What is not in dispute: somatic and co-regulation practices produce effects that do not depend on polyvagal being mechanistically correct.

**Actionable:** Pull the language audit on the Waystone site and your intake materials. Anywhere you say ventral or dorsal as if it were settled anatomy, rewrite it as what a client actually reports: settled, revved, gone flat. Describe the state, not the nerve.

**Why this matters to you:** Twenty-five years on, the bill is still coming. What lands here is not the exposure finding, which is old news, but the PTSD finding: the responders carrying PTSD showed brains roughly three years older than their age, and the ones without it did not. Untreated post-traumatic stress is not only a quality-of-life problem. It appears to be structural.

**The story:** Researchers imaged World Trade Center responders and used a model trained on more than 11,500 MRI scans to estimate brain age from structure. Responders with PTSD showed estimated brain ages about three years above their chronological age. Responders without PTSD tracked their actual age. Prolonged Ground Zero exposure was independently associated with the same pattern. Around 23 percent of WTC responders carry a PTSD diagnosis, against roughly 6 percent lifetime prevalence in the general U.S. population. Many describe worsening memory, concentration, and sharpness as they age.

**Actionable:** When a responder tells you the fog is age, treat it as a question, not a fact. Ask what else has been running that long.
