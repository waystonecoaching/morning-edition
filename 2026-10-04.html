Assessing the feasibility and acceptability of training first responder peer supporters in trauma risk management (TRiM)

Peer-reviewed work on training responders as peer supporters in TRiM, a structured post-incident approach out of the UK military and emergency services. Feasibility and acceptability are the early questions, not outcomes, so this says people will do it, not that it works. It speaks to the exact lane Phoenix Fire peer programs live in. Scope edge: TRiM is screening and triage after potentially traumatic events. It is not treatment and not coaching.

The study asks whether first responders can be trained as TRiM peer supporters and whether the program is acceptable to them. The feed carries no abstract, so sample, setting and results are not summarized here. Treat it as a feasibility signal until you read the numbers.

Try this: Get the full text and note what the referral pathway out of TRiM looks like. That is where a coach either has a place or does not.
Source: Journal of Traumatic Stress

Compass Shares 52-Week Follow-Up and Five Training Grant Recipients (Psychedelic Alpha Bulletin #231)

Company-reported topline from Part C of COMP005, the 258-patient placebo-controlled Phase 3 of COMP360 psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression. It is depression, not PTSD, and it is the sponsor talking, so wait for the peer-reviewed paper. The training grant recipients are the part closer to your path, since who gets trained is who ends up doing the work.

Compass shared topline data from Part C of COMP005, which enrolled 258 TRD patients to a 25 mg dose of COMP360 or inert placebo, with a 52-week follow-up. The bulletin also lists five training grant recipients, plus a new compound entering the clinic and an RFK Jr. event. Durability at one year is the number to look for once the full data is out.

Try this: Read the grant recipient list and see which training bodies are getting funded.

A single dose of MDMA prevents trauma-fueled drinking in genetically prone rats

Rats. Preclinical, so it tells you about mechanism and risk stratification, not about a firefighter at 0300 with a bottle. Still, trauma plus alcohol is the actual presentation in your caseload, and the genetic-susceptibility angle is a fair question. Do not let a rodent result get repeated as a treatment claim. Scope edge: substance use, clinical territory.

PsyPost reports a study in which one dose of MDMA prevented trauma-driven alcohol drinking in rats bred or selected as genetically prone to it. I read the headline and feed entry only. Species, dose and timing relative to the trauma are not confirmed here.

Try this: If a client asks, say it is early animal work. Nothing more.
