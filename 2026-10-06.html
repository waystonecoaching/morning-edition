The interoceptive communication model (ICM 2.0): a state-dependent neurophysiological framework for behavioral and cognitive accessibility

A state-dependent model of why access to skills and insight swings with autonomic and interoceptive state. That is close to the substrate claim in your polyvagal frame: felt safety before transformation. It is a single new framework paper in Frontiers, not a replication, so hold it as a proposal.

Frontiers in Psychology proposes ICM 2.0, a neurophysiological framework for why behavioral, emotional and cognitive performance varies across contexts even when knowledge and skills are intact. It points to trauma-related conditions as a common site of that variability.

Try this: Check whether its language for state-dependent access is cleaner than what you use with clients.
