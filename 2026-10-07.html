Pα+ Psychedelic Bulletin #231: Compass Shares 52 Week Follow-Up, Training Grant Recipients; Xylo Enters the Clinic; RFK Jr. Hosts Hubbard

Read this one for the credibility question, not the headline. COMP005 is psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression, not PTSD, so it does not move the first responder standard of care. It does show what durable-effect claims look like when a trial runs open-label after the blind breaks. Psychedelic-adjacent, clinical scope.

Compass Pathways reported Part C of COMP005, a 258-patient placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial of 25 mg COMP360 in treatment-resistant depression. Participants who got the active dose first showed an average 13-point MADRS reduction from baseline at Week 52 after a second dose. About 70 percent (179 of 258) entered the open-label Part C, dropout was roughly equal across groups, and the reasons for discontinuation have not been disclosed. Psychedelic Alpha also covers five training grant recipients.

Try this: When a client quotes a 52-week number, ask whether it came from the blinded phase or the open-label one.

Effects of a brief resonance frequency breathing intervention on heart rate variability and perceived stress in postgraduate students: a pilot and feasibility study

A small, honest pilot on slow-paced breathing and autonomic regulation. HRV moved, felt stress did not reach significance. That gap is the useful part: the body can shift before the story does. Polyvagal-adjacent, though the paper is about HRV, not polyvagal theory.

Twelve postgraduate students were randomized to six or six. The intervention group did ten minutes a day of app-guided resonance frequency breathing for three weeks. Between-group differences favored breathing on SDNN at weeks 2 and 3 and on RMSSD at week 3. Perceived stress showed no significant difference (p=0.199), though the intervention group dropped about 6 points while controls rose 4.5. Limits: tiny sample, no active control, no follow-up, self-reported adherence.

Try this: Try ten minutes of paced breathing yourself for a week and note what changes in the body before the story changes.
Source: Frontiers in Psychology
