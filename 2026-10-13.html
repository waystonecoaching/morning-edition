A living systematic review, meta-analysis, and open data resource of trials of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD

This is the anchor for the issue. A living meta-analysis with open data is the right instrument for a field under credibility stress, because anyone can re-run the numbers as trials land. Read the pooled effect next to the known problems: functional unblinding, thin adverse-event capture, and a sponsor lineage that has already taken a rejection. Clinical-adjacent. This is a standard-of-care question, not a coaching one.

Published online 11 October in Neuropsychopharmacology. It pools trials of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD into a living systematic review and meta-analysis and releases the underlying data as an open resource. The feed carries only the title, and the journal page was rate-limited, so effect sizes and certainty ratings are not summarized here. Pull the paper before quoting a number.

Blinding Integrity and Psychedelic Drug Trials - JAMA

Blinding is where the whole field is soft. If participants can tell drug from placebo, expectancy does part of the work, and effect sizes inflate. JAMA is treating it as its own topic, which tells you where the critique has landed. Hold every large effect size in this space against that.

A JAMA piece on blinding integrity in psychedelic drug trials, published 12 October. The feed gives the title only, so the specific recommendations are not summarized here. The subject is the standing problem that vivid subjective effects break the blind, and what trial design can do about it.

Try this: Ask of any psychedelic result you read this week: how was the blind tested, and what did the participants guess.

MAPS Responds to Report of Progress for MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD with FDA

MAPS answering a Psychedelic Alpha report that Resilient Pharmaceuticals, formerly MAPS PBC and Lykos, has resubmitted its MDMA application. Read it as advocacy from an interested party. The useful part is what it concedes: partial functional unblinding and adverse events not fully captured at the time. This is the credibility stress, stated by the people who ran the trials.

MAPS says the resubmission went in without a new Phase 3 study and that the 2024 FDA Complete Response Letter asked about durability, safety characterization and bias reduction. It calls the FDA's July 2026 psychedelic trial guidance a reflection of approaches MAPS developed with the agency, and says it has no active role in Resilient's drug development. It is restating a third-party report, not confirming the filing. A resubmission is not an approval.

Try this: Watch for the FDA's filing acceptance and review timeline. That date matters more than the statement.
Source: MAPS
