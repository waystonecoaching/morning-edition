**Source:** Neuropsychopharmacology (Nature)  \
**Category:** psychedelic  \

**Link:** [https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-026-02575-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-026-02575-z)

**Why this matters to you:** This is the cleanest read on where MDMA for PTSD actually stands, and it is more sober than the approval chatter. Pooled effect is real, certainty is low, and the authors say so. Read it against the field's credibility problem, not as a victory lap. Dosing, screening and monitoring sit well inside clinical scope. Your lane is what a client does with the experience afterward.

**The story:** A living systematic review and meta-analysis of six randomized trials of MDMA-assisted therapy for PTSD, 286 participants in all. Pooled effect on primary PTSD symptoms was Hedges' g of -0.71, with a response risk ratio of 1.35 and a remission risk ratio of 2.25 against placebo. More dosing sessions and higher cumulative dose tracked with larger effects. Most trials rated low risk of bias, but overall certainty of evidence was graded low, and the authors note the bias tool misses expectancy effects, functional unblinding, conduct problems and safety. Benefits are short-term. The database is open and will update as trials land.

**Actionable:** Pull the open data dashboard (sypres.io) and read the expectancy and unblinding caveats before you next discuss MDMA outcomes with a client or a colleague.
