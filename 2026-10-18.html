Pα+ Practitioner Issue 7: Preparedness (Systems)

Trade-press, not trial data, but it is practitioners talking about what breaks after approval: rooms, scheduling, paperwork, payment. Useful reality check on the distance between a green light and a working service. Treat advice from people selling or building in the space as field notes, not evidence. Prep, dosing and monitoring are clinical. Your lane starts when the session ends.

The issue argues practitioners should prepare the system before the first patient: physical space, REMS compliance, logistics and reimbursement. Six-hour psilocybin sessions make room planning, bathroom access and scheduling real constraints. Reimbursement codes are still Category III, and conversion could take 12 to 18 months, so contributors advise negotiating coverage and planning a slow launch. They also say to start small, measure outcomes from day one and plan for rural and lower-income access.

Try this: If you take integration referrals from a psychedelic clinic, ask how they plan to hand clients off, and who holds the client between sessions.
Source: Psychedelic Alpha

MAPS Attends Historic FDA Hearing on Psychedelics

Advocacy source, so read it as MAPS's account of the room and not a neutral record. The useful part is what it says the hearing left open: workforce, informed consent and insurance, which is where integration practitioners get defined or left out. This is also an organization with a credibility history, so weigh its claims against the docket. Therapist training and post-treatment support touch your scope directly.

The FDA held its public hearing on future therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs on September 14, following final guidance on trial design, blinding and long-term safety follow-up. MAPS says that guidance left workforce, consent and insurance open, and that speakers took those up. It is preparing written testimony on systems readiness, therapist training capacity and patient access, with the docket open to October 5. Its leaders argue that clinical data matters little if licensing, insurance codes and community support are not built, and that integration should live in communities as well as clinics.

Try this: Read the FDA docket comments on therapist training and see where integration support is named or missing.
Source: MAPS
