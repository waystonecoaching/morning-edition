Ketamine trial targets PTSD in first responders

One of the few trial headlines aimed at responders as a population, not veterans by default. Ketamine sits outside the MDMA credibility fight, but it is still clinical delivery with dissociation and abuse liability. Scope edge: prescribing and supervised dosing are not coaching work. Your lane is preparation and integration around it.

Per the headline and feed stub, an Australian trial is testing ketamine for PTSD in first responders. I could not open the full text, so sample size, design, dose and control arm are unread. Treat it as a pointer to chase, not a finding.

Try this: Find the trial registry entry and check whether it runs a psychotherapy component or ketamine alone.

Ketamine and MDMA treatments are linked to epigenetic changes in genes tied to brain plasticity

Mechanism work, not outcome work. An epigenetic change tied to plasticity genes does not show a patient got better or stayed better. Read with that gap held open, since mechanism headlines are where the field tends to over-promise.

Per the headline, a study links ketamine and MDMA treatment to epigenetic changes in genes involved in brain plasticity. Design, sample and effect size are unread.

Source: PsyPost
