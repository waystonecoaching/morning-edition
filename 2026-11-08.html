MAPS Attends Historic FDA Hearing on Psychedelics

The FDA held a public hearing on 14 September on psychedelic therapy, and the open questions were workforce, informed consent and insurance, not efficacy. That is the part that decides whether integration practitioners ever get a lane. MAPS is an interested party here, and this is its own account, so read it as advocacy with a useful list of what is unsettled. Therapist training and readiness sit adjacent to your scope, and the clinical work stays with licensed prescribers.

MAPS says it attended the 14 September FDA hearing and plans to file written testimony on systems readiness, therapist training and patient access. It also handed HHS, FDA and VA its open-science ibogaine investigator's brochure. It frames FDA approval as necessary but not enough for access, and urges training, insurance and community support to be built now. The page makes no claims about MDMA or PTSD outcomes. The comment deadline it cites, 5 October, has passed.

Try this: Check whether the docket's readiness and training comments name an integration or peer role. That is where a BTTY-trained practitioner would appear on paper, if anywhere.

Ketamine trial targets PTSD in first responders

A ketamine trial aimed at first responders with PTSD, from ABC Australia. Ketamine sits in a different place than MDMA, with a faster signal and a shorter track record in PTSD specifically, so do not borrow MDMA's evidence for it. This is clinical and high-acuity territory, and comorbid substance use and suicidality are the usual exclusions and the usual risk. Nothing here changes what a coach does.

I could only read the headline and feed snippet; the Google News redirect refused the fetch. The headline says a ketamine trial is targeting PTSD in first responders. I have no sample size, dosing, control arm, site or endpoints, so I cannot say whether it is a pilot or a powered trial. Treat it as a pointer to chase down.

Try this: Find the trial registration and read the inclusion and exclusion criteria. They tell you more about the population than the news item will.
