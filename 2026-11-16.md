**Why this matters to you:** Bill Nash wrote the Marine Corps combat stress doctrine, and here he gives Shay's old claim a mechanism: moral injury as a torn attachment, not a fear response. That sits close to your own frame. Felt safety and relationship come first, and the wound is relational before it is cognitive. Pure theory, untested, so use it as language rather than evidence.

**The story:** William Nash's moral engine model, published in Frontiers in Psychiatry and covered by Bioengineer, defines moral injury as a wound to the self when a betrayal of moral trust ruptures defining and sustaining attachments. Drawing on Kohut's self psychology, the model criticizes the DSM's fear-centered view of PTSD for sidelining shame and anger. Its treatment implications are tentative: offer a steady valuing relationship, clear away obstacles like dissociation and substance use, and repair or replace what was broken, including amends where possible.
