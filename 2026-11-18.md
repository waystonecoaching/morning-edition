### 01. For Military Veterans with PTSD and Alcohol Use Disorder, MDMA-Assisted Therapy May Be Safe, Effective

**Why this matters to you:** PTSD and the bottle travel together in the firehouse more often than either one travels alone, and most trials throw out the drinkers. This one took them in. Read it for what it is: ten men, no control arm, four weeks of follow-up. The PTSD drop looks big and the drinking drop does not hold up even by the authors' own reckoning. This sits square on the substance use edge, so keep it in your reading and out of your referrals for now.

**The story:** A Brown University team, published in the journal Alcohol, ran ten veterans with both PTSD and alcohol use disorder through eleven sessions over five months: three preparation sessions, two MDMA sessions of up to eight hours, and six integration sessions. Nine finished. Mean CAPS-5 scores fell by more than 22 points, moving the average participant from severe to mild, and no serious adverse events were reported. Drinks per drinking day fell 31 percent and heavy drinking days fell 54 percent, but the researchers did not call that clinically significant, partly because some men cut back before the trial began. The next step is a randomized trial with the Providence VA against a low-dose active control.

**Actionable:** When a client brings you this headline, hand him the numbers: ten men, no control group, one month out. Then ask what he is hoping the medicine will do that the work has not.

### 03. Using Multimodal Passive Workplace Technologies for Unobtrusive Employee Stress, Anxiety and Burnout Monitoring: A Systematic Review

**Source:** Frontiers in Psychology  \
**Category:** first_responder_mh  \
**Published:** 2026-11-18  \
**Link:** [https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.2011664](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.2011664)

**Why this matters to you:** Somebody is going to sell a fire department a stress dashboard built on keystrokes, eye gaze, and thermal cameras. This review is the thing to hand the battalion chief when they do. It sits in the operational camp of the resilience field, measure and flag, and its own conclusion is that the measuring is not ready. Read it alongside the betrayal research: a crew that feels watched will not tell you the truth, and no sensor fixes that.

**The story:** The authors screened 973 records and kept 15 peer-reviewed studies from 2011 to 2025 on detecting occupational stress, anxiety, and burnout through keystroke and mouse dynamics, eye gaze, facial thermography, and body posture. All four methods showed feasibility for spotting stress, and multimodal models generally beat single signals. No included study measured burnout directly and only one measured anxiety. Samples were small and mostly lab-based, few studies described any ethical safeguards or governance, and the authors conclude the evidence is insufficient to support routine workplace use.

**Actionable:** If a department wellness pitch crosses your desk with passive monitoring in it, ask two questions: who sees the data, and what happens to the man it flags.
