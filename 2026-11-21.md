### 01. Essentials of Ethical Research With Participants at Risk for Suicide

**Source:** Center for the Study of Traumatic Stress (USU)  \
**Category:** first_responder_mh  \
**Published:** 2026-11-15  \
**Link:** [https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiiAFBVV95cUxQaDF2amRNcGZMRGhmOGFMRlJ5enVrZGdrZGxxSkxjQXYySmg5YzdMT2M1VlZId2JvZTZlMFh0Ym44eFVudFc0U1JQMThzaHo4VS0ycmtEZFd1TXIyMU5vVzlidU41U0tEbEw3Si1OQmdybmtaYXMzYmdrSmhzT0Znd0NpMDNEOHNp?oc=5](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiiAFBVV95cUxQaDF2amRNcGZMRGhmOGFMRlJ5enVrZGdrZGxxSkxjQXYySmg5YzdMT2M1VlZId2JvZTZlMFh0Ym44eFVudFc0U1JQMThzaHo4VS0ycmtEZFd1TXIyMU5vVzlidU41U0tEbEw3Si1OQmdybmtaYXMzYmdrSmhzT0Znd0NpMDNEOHNp?oc=5)

**Why this matters to you:** Every first responder suicide study you will cite later starts with a researcher sitting across from someone who might be at risk, deciding what to ask and what to do with the answer. This CSTS project is building the training for that moment. It matters to you two ways: it is the standard you can hold any responder survey or peer-support evaluation to before you lend your name to it, and the same questions (when to stop, when to escalate, who holds the risk) are the ones you work on a crisis scene. Suicidality is a scope edge for a coach. This is the research side of that edge, not a coaching protocol.

**The story:** The Suicide Prevention Program at the Center for the Study of Traumatic Stress, Uniformed Services University, is developing an e-learning curriculum to teach researchers in varied settings how to assess and manage suicide risk during human-subjects research. The project page ties the work to CDC suicide-prevention strategy and federal military and veteran suicide priorities, and draws on NIMH guidance for research with participants at elevated risk. Marjan Holloway and Jessica LaCroix are named on the project. The page gives no launch date, module count, or cost, so this is a project in development, not a course you can take today.

**Actionable:** Before you join or promote any responder survey or program evaluation this year, ask the team one question: what is your protocol when a participant discloses active ideation? If they cannot answer in a sentence, do not put your name on it.

**Why this matters to you:** This is the human-scale close. Tate carried the badge for four decades, much of it through addiction, and got sober with faith and AA. That is the arc a lot of your clients are in the middle of and cannot yet say out loud. Substance use is a scope edge: you walk beside the recovery, you do not run it. I could not open the TribLIVE piece itself; the details below come from earlier coverage of the same memoir.

**The story:** TribLIVE's Offbeat Pittsburgh column features Brenda Tate and her memoir, Journal of a Black Woman in Blue, subtitled Navigating Abuse, Addiction, Racism and Sobriety. Earlier coverage in the New Pittsburgh Courier and Police1 reports that Tate served 40 years with the Pittsburgh Bureau of Police, joining in the 1970s, worked witness protection and dignitary protection, and was the only African American in the dignitary unit at the time. The Courier reports that roughly 20 of those years were heavily affected by drugs and alcohol, and that she got sober through faith and Alcoholics Anonymous. Her life was also the subject of a 2024 PBS documentary.

**Actionable:** Read the book before you recommend it. If it holds up, it may be the one a retiring officer will actually pick up, because it is a cop telling it and not a clinician.
