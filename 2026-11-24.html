AMA Names Moral Injury a Distinct Threat to Physician Well-Being

Structural camp, stated by the biggest medical body in the country: moral injury comes from the system, and coping skills will not fix it. That sentence is the same argument you make about shift culture and command decisions. It gives you an outside authority when a chief hears moral injury and reaches for a resilience class.

On November 9, 2026, the AMA House of Delegates adopted policy calling moral injury a distinct and significant threat to physicians, residents, and medical students. The AMA separates three things: burnout as exhaustion and lost fulfillment, moral distress as being blocked from doing what one believes is right, and moral injury as what follows when those conflicts persist and erode meaning and identity. It says the three can overlap. The policy directs the AMA to develop recommendations and says well-being needs systems reform rather than individual coping. No timeline was given.

Try this: Keep the three definitions on one card. Use them to sort what a client is carrying before you pick a frame.
Source: American Medical Association
