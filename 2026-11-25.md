**Why this matters to you:** This is the field checking its own math, and the math came back thin. The animal work under the psilocybin story was supposed to show lasting change. Run the same way in five labs, it mostly did not. That does not settle anything for humans, but it is one more reason to read psychedelic headlines with your hand on your wallet. The field is under credibility stress and this is part of why.

**The story:** Five labs at UC Berkeley, UC San Francisco and Stanford ran identical psilocybin protocols in mice, published in Nature Neuroscience and covered here by ZME Science. Pooled, the most reproducible effects were a spike in anxiety-like behavior right after dosing and reduced fear expression in the acute window. Lasting reductions in depression- or anxiety-like behavior did not replicate. The researchers put the inconsistency down to a small underlying effect, and ruled out handler sex, mouse supplier and housing time. Co-first author Odilia Lu notes the limits of translating mouse behavior models to human depression.

**Actionable:** When a client quotes a psilocybin result, ask one question first: was it people or mice, and has anyone run it twice?
