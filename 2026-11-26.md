**Why this matters to you:** This is the first late-stage psilocybin trial aimed at PTSD, and it is the one that will decide whether COMP360 gets anywhere near a responder's chart. Read the source for what it is: Australian small-cap press written for shareholders of the clinic, not the science. The trial design is what matters. Week eight is the primary endpoint, and eight weeks is a short look at a wound that runs for years. PTSD with suicidality and substance use sits right at the edge of scope; this is clinical territory, and it stays there.

**The story:** Compass Pathways picked Emyria's Empax Centre in Leederville, Western Australia, as a site for COMP202, the Redefine Study. The primary measure is change in PTSD symptom severity from baseline to week eight. Emyria's target is up to 32 participants, with recruitment set to start in December 2026 pending ethics approval and site activation. Emyria will run screening, preparation, dosing-day monitoring and follow-up, and it expects about $1.5 million in revenue from 20 completed participants. **Actionable:** When a client asks about psilocybin for PTSD, the honest answer this week is: one late-stage trial is starting, no results yet, and none of it is available outside a study.

### 03. Teaching the Body to Stand Down: An Embodied Mindfulness Model for Sleep

**Source:** Frontiers in Psychology  \
**Category:** polyvagal  \
**Published:** 2026-11-26  \
**Link:** [https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1996051](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1996051)

**Why this matters to you:** Every man on shift knows the bunk-room problem: tired to the bone and still wired at 0300. This paper says the trouble is a body that will not de-arouse, and that breath joined to slow movement may teach it faster than sitting still. That is close kin to the old practice of the prayer set to the breath, and close kin to the autonomic language you already use. It is a hypothesis paper, not a trial. The authors say so themselves.

**The story:** Sunny Chan (University of the West of England) and Samuel Nyman (University of Winchester) propose an Embodied Mindfulness Framework for insomnia, using Tai Chi as the model practice. They define embodied mindfulness as present-centered, nonjudgmental awareness built through coordinated attention, breathing, posture, bodily sensation and intentional movement. The framework holds that autonomic flexibility, interoceptive reappraisal and attentional anchoring work together, so that arousal is felt without spiraling into worry or effort to sleep. The authors grant that current Tai Chi evidence cannot yet separate these mechanisms from plain exercise, expectancy or social contact. They position it alongside CBT-I, not as a replacement, and call for dismantling and mediation studies.

**Actionable:** For a client who cannot come down after nights, try one slow breath-paced movement routine before bed for two weeks, and have him note sleep onset. Insomnia that persists past that is a referral to CBT-I, not a coaching problem.
