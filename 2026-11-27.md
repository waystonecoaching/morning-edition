**Why this matters to you:** Read this as testimony, not evidence. Van der Kolk worked on the MAPS phase 3 trials and says he has been on both sides of the fight; this is part two of a sit-down with Joseph Goldberg, and every number in it comes from his memory. He says the 38 hours of therapy alone did little. The published MAPP1 numbers show the placebo-plus-therapy arm improving too, only less, so that line is his read, not the data's. **The story:** Goldberg and van der Kolk discuss psychedelic-assisted therapy for PTSD. **Actionable:** When a client brings this interview in, ask what they heard it promise. Then hand them the 2024 FDA decision and let the two sit side by side.

**The story:** CSTS at the Uniformed Services University advises colleagues to reach out directly without waiting to be asked, since perfect words are not required, and to keep support ongoing rather than one-time. **Published:** 2026-11-26  \

**Why this matters to you:** The finding is autonomic even though the paper never says polyvagal: it is arousal, good or bad, that makes the cue bite harder. That maps onto the end of a shift. A big save and a bad call both leave a man running hot, and the bar on the way home reads differently in that state. Small sample, secondhand write-up of a Brown University study, and substance use is scope-edge work. Still worth the read.

**The story:** Researchers tracked 33 adults with alcohol use disorder by smartphone over 28 days, sampling mood, surroundings and craving in real time. High-arousal positive mood strengthened the link between seeing alcohol and craving it. High-arousal negative mood strengthened the link between being in familiar drinking places and craving. The write-up notes that fewer than one in five people with lifetime alcohol use disorder ever reach formal treatment or mutual-help groups, and that 60 to 70% of treatment completers return to heavy drinking within three months.

**Actionable:** Ask a client which state they were in the last three times they drank after shift: wired-up good, wired-up bad, or flat. Map the cue to the state, not just the place.

**Source:** Bioengineer.org  \
**Category:** coaching  \
**Published:** 2026-11-23  \
**Link:** [https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiugFBVV95cUxOUjNyWUk4SjNndl80XzRaY21pS1VtX0Y1bFpBR1oyOVd3M1dIdUs5ei03VlUwaUY3YnBURGpXaVZoX09zOFlaWl9BTGM4Vm9nUUEyOTdWQzY5WTNPSHk1Ykw2X3BLN19KVWRuemYxWmtleHFuUklVLXF3YU1xbGd2NC02Wnc0VWlEdkwwbWkxSWFDTG1sVEZJa3JkVzlJYWdZdUxKdnNfblQzQVB0Qjl0WFF0WW9FTnhSa3c?oc=5](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiugFBVV95cUxOUjNyWUk4SjNndl80XzRaY21pS1VtX0Y1bFpBR1oyOVd3M1dIdUs5ei03VlUwaUY3YnBURGpXaVZoX09zOFlaWl9BTGM4Vm9nUUEyOTdWQzY5WTNPSHk1Ykw2X3BLN19KVWRuemYxWmtleHFuUklVLXF3YU1xbGd2NC02Wnc0VWlEdkwwbWkxSWFDTG1sVEZJa3JkVzlJYWdZdUxKdnNfblQzQVB0Qjl0WFF0WW9FTnhSa3c?oc=5)

**Why this matters to you:** One conversation built on motivational interviewing moved drinking for six months. By two years the effect was gone. For a solo practice that is the whole lesson in one trial: a single good session opens a door, and the door swings shut without follow-up. Small sample, women in São Paulo primary care, so carry it to responders with care.

**The story:** A single-blind randomized trial in four São Paulo primary care clinics enrolled 151 women scoring 8 to 19 on the AUDIT. Nine nurses with 20 hours of remote training delivered a ten-minute phone session built on the FRAMES model: feedback, responsibility, advice, a menu of options, empathy and self-efficacy. Controls got brief feedback only. The intervention group reached its lowest drinking scores at six months, about two points down, while controls drifted upward. By 24 months the advantage had faded. Published in Archives of Women's Mental Health.

**Actionable:** After any first session that lands, book the check-in before the client leaves. The trial says the drop-off is real.


### 06. Most Veterans With PTSD Are on Five or More Medications, Month After Month

**Source:** Journal of Traumatic Stress  \
**Category:** first_responder_mh  \
**Published:** 2026-11-27  \
**Link:** [https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70145?af=R](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jts.70145?af=R)

**Why this matters to you:** Veterans, not firefighters, but the pharmacy cabinet looks the same in a lot of retired responder homes. When a third of a client's day goes to managing pills, that is part of the field you are coaching into. One in ten here had benzodiazepines co-dispensed with another psychotropic month after month. You do not touch the meds. You can ask who last reviewed them.

**The story:** Researchers examined Department of Veterans' Affairs data on 24,923 ex-serving Australian Defence Force veterans with accepted PTSD from June 2018 to May 2023. 59.8% met the definition of general polypharmacy (five or more drugs in a month, in at least five months) and 21.8% met psychotropic polypharmacy (two or more psychotropics). Average exposure ran 32.9 and 26.1 months. Each ten years of age raised the probability of general polypharmacy by 11.3% and lowered psychotropic polypharmacy by 3.6%. Repeated benzodiazepine co-dispensing occurred in 10.1% and antipsychotic co-dispensing in 7.4%. The authors call for ongoing medication review.

**Actionable:** Add one intake question: when did a prescriber last review everything you take, all at once?

**Why this matters to you:** A day after Thanksgiving, with the house still and the pager quiet, a lot of men will find they cannot sit down. This piece names why. The author is a trauma recovery coach who works with women survivors of sexual abuse, so mind the scope; her statistics are loosely sourced and the outlet is popular, not peer-reviewed. Her central line is worth keeping: you cannot explain safety to a nervous system, you can only show it. The fathers would recognize that. Stillness is not ordered into a man. It is given in small portions, until he can bear it.

**The story:** Jean Dorff describes a client who scheduled an empty weekend and spent it cleaning, a body treating stillness as lost readiness. She argues that a nervous system shaped by threat reads rest as exposure, so willpower and advice to unplug rarely work. She calls hypervigilance a precise awareness that outlived its context and argues the body updates on felt evidence, not reassurance: feet on the floor, a longer exhale, shoulders dropping. Rest returns in small, verifiable steps, alongside trauma-informed therapy.

**Actionable:** With the next client who says he cannot rest, skip the advice. Ask for two minutes of stillness in session and track what the body does first.
